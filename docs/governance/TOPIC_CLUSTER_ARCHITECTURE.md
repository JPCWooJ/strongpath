# TOPIC_CLUSTER_ARCHITECTURE.md

## Purpose

Canonical Layer 2 topical authority and content-cluster architecture system for StrongPath.

This file defines how StrongPath organizes topics into clusters, pillars, satellites, boundaries, intent maps, persona pathways, maturity stages, refresh rules, prioritization, merges, splits, and authority-building decisions.

This is an editorial governance document. It does not define CMS implementation systems, analytics/reporting systems, programmatic SEO systems, AI content-farm architectures, or enterprise taxonomy bureaucracy.

## 1. Core Principle

StrongPath clusters exist to build trust through depth, clarity, and restraint.

A cluster is not a keyword bucket. It is an organized answer system for a reader problem StrongPath can address credibly. Every cluster should help readers understand where they are, what matters next, and which claims StrongPath can support.

## 2. Cluster-Definition Standards

A cluster must have:

- clear reader problem
- primary persona or persona mix
- durable topic boundary
- parent pillar or planned pillar
- supporting satellite roles
- search-intent map
- evidence requirements
- internal-link pathway
- commerce and book boundaries, if relevant
- refresh owner or maintenance expectation

Do not create a cluster around a single keyword when the underlying reader need is too thin, unsafe, or outside StrongPath scope.

## 3. Pillar-Definition Standards

A pillar is the authoritative entry point for a cluster.

Pillars must:

- answer the broad topic clearly
- define key terms and boundaries
- cite deeply enough for the topic risk
- link to the most useful satellites
- preserve evidence and commerce separation
- establish the cluster's reader pathway
- avoid overclaiming for search visibility
- support future refreshes and internal expansion

Pillar approval requires a brief, research packet when needed, internal-link plan, QA, scoring, and founder review when required.

## 4. Satellite-Definition Standards

A satellite answers a focused question inside a cluster.

Satellites must:

- serve one primary intent
- support or clarify the parent pillar
- link back to the parent pillar
- stay narrower than the pillar
- avoid duplicating sibling articles
- cite claims appropriate to risk
- provide one useful next step

A satellite may bridge clusters only when the brief names the primary cluster and explains the secondary connection.

## 5. Cluster Expansion Rules

Expand a cluster when:

- pillar is current and trustworthy
- existing satellites do not answer a real reader need
- search intent is distinct
- evidence can support the topic
- internal links can route readers clearly
- new article strengthens authority rather than scattering it

Do not expand when:

- pillar is stale
- topic overlaps an existing article
- claim territory is unsafe
- content would be thin
- expansion exists only to capture keyword variants
- commerce pressure is driving the topic before education

Depth comes before breadth.

## 6. Authority-Depth Requirements

Authority depth means the cluster has enough coverage to feel complete for its intended scope.

Minimum depth signals:

- pillar defines the core problem
- satellites answer execution, safety, mechanism, persona, and objection questions
- citations match claim risk
- internal links connect the cluster
- update policy is clear
- book or product pathways are properly separated

Do not call a cluster mature because it has many pages. A mature cluster has useful, maintained, non-duplicative pages.

## 7. Cluster Ownership Rules

Each cluster should have one primary editorial owner by function.

Cluster owner responsibilities:

- define topic boundary
- maintain pillar/satellite map
- flag overlap
- guide briefs
- confirm internal-link pathway
- monitor refresh needs
- route escalations

One person may own multiple clusters, and one person may perform multiple functions. Ownership means the next decision is not ambiguous.

## 8. Overlap-Prevention Rules

Overlap is prevented at brief stage.

Before approving a new article, check:

- existing pillar coverage
- existing satellite coverage
- planned articles
- search intent
- persona moment
- claim territory
- internal-link role

If two articles would answer the same reader in the same way, merge the idea, narrow one article, or reject the weaker topic.

Overlap is not solved by changing the title if the answer is still the same.

## 9. Topic-Boundary Governance

Topic boundaries protect StrongPath from drift.

A cluster boundary must state:

- what belongs inside the cluster
- what belongs in a neighboring cluster
- what is out of scope
- what claims require escalation
- what language is unsafe or brand-inconsistent
- where commerce may appear

Boundary examples:

- sarcopenia cluster may explain age-related muscle loss, strength, protein, and independence
- it should not make disease-treatment, cure, reversal, or clinical protocol claims
- supplement-adjacent content requires commerce-sensitive review

When boundaries are unclear, narrow the topic before drafting.

## 10. Search-Intent Mapping

Each cluster needs an intent map.

Intent map fields:

- awareness questions
- definition questions
- mechanism questions
- execution questions
- safety questions
- comparison questions
- caregiver questions
- commercial-support questions
- navigational/book questions

Assign each intent to one best page. Do not create multiple pages for the same intent unless persona, risk, or action pathway is genuinely different.

## 11. Persona-To-Cluster Mapping

Clusters should identify which personas they serve and how.

Mapping should define:

- primary persona
- secondary persona constraints
- persona-specific language to use or avoid
- likely emotional state
- next-step pathway
- inappropriate pathways

Examples:

- Margaret needs peer-level clarity and practical control, not "elderly" framing.
- David needs reduced overwhelm and parent-safety pathways, not fear escalation.
- Dr. Chen needs citation discipline and clear limits, not consumer urgency.

Persona mapping governs internal links, CTAs, and article briefs.

## 12. Cluster Maturity Stages

Use one maturity stage per cluster.

| Stage | Meaning | Next Action |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate | Topic may become a cluster. | Validate reader need, evidence, and scope. |
| Seed | Initial pillar or key article exists. | Build brief map and first satellites. |
| Emerging | Pillar plus several useful satellites exist. | Improve links, evidence, and coverage gaps. |
| Mature | Cluster is coherent, current, and internally linked. | Maintain and refresh. |
| Refresh Needed | Cluster has stale claims, links, or structure. | Apply `CONTENT_UPDATE_POLICY.md`. |
| Merge/Split Needed | Cluster boundary is unclear or overlapping. | Run merge/split review. |
| Deprecated | Cluster no longer belongs in active strategy. | Archive or redirect active pathways. |

Do not expand aggressively before a cluster reaches `Emerging`.

## 13. Cluster Refresh Governance

Cluster refresh follows `CONTENT_UPDATE_POLICY.md`.

Refresh cluster when:

- pillar refresh is due
- multiple satellites are stale
- citations need revalidation
- internal links no longer reflect the best pathway
- new article creates overlap
- governance changes affect claim or commerce rules
- cluster positioning changes

Cluster refresh should check the map first, then individual articles. Otherwise the work becomes scattered patching.

## 14. Cluster Scoring And Prioritization

Prioritize clusters with a simple editorial score.

Score each factor from 1 to 5:

- reader need
- StrongPath authority fit
- evidence support
- persona importance
- search opportunity
- commerce sensitivity risk, scored lower when risk is higher
- existing coverage gap
- maintenance burden, scored lower when burden is higher

High-priority clusters have strong reader need, strong authority fit, supportable evidence, and clear pathways.

Do not prioritize a cluster only because search volume is high.

## 15. Orphan-Cluster Prevention

No cluster should exist without a pathway into the rest of StrongPath.

Every active cluster should have:

- relationship to at least one major StrongPath authority theme
- incoming link from a relevant pillar, homepage path, book page, or related cluster
- outgoing links to related education or next-step pages
- clear distinction from neighboring clusters
- maintenance state

If a cluster cannot be connected without forcing links, reassess whether it belongs.

## 16. Merge And Split Rules

Merge clusters when:

- they serve the same reader problem
- boundaries are artificial
- pillars compete for the same intent
- internal links confuse authority flow
- one cluster is too thin to stand alone

Split a cluster when:

- one pillar is trying to serve multiple major intents
- personas require meaningfully different pathways
- evidence or safety rules differ sharply
- commerce-sensitive content needs separation
- satellite scope has grown into its own authority area

Merge/split output must name surviving pillars, redirected articles, revised briefs, internal-link changes, and founder-review need.

## 17. Strategic Authority-Building Logic

StrongPath authority should build around a few durable themes before expanding outward.

Strategic cluster families:

- sarcopenia and age-related muscle loss
- strength as independence
- resistance training execution
- protein, recovery, and progression
- caregiver support and parent-strength guidance
- book and brand-term defense
- safety, late-start, and older-adult adaptation

Each new cluster should strengthen at least one of:

- topical authority
- reader trust
- citation record
- internal-link clarity
- book/brand credibility
- safe commercial pathway

Publishing more pages is not the goal. Building a coherent body of trustworthy guidance is the goal.

## 18. Escalation Rules

Escalate when:

- cluster boundary is disputed
- pillar claim territory is risky
- keyword opportunity pressures unsafe wording
- new topic overlaps active content
- merge or split affects a pillar
- cluster creates commerce or affiliate pressure
- cluster would target crisis, clinical, or out-of-scope intent
- persona pathway is unclear or emotionally sensitive
- internal links cannot connect the cluster honestly
- founder, book, author, product, quiz, or program framing is affected

Escalation output must state:

- cluster
- issue
- affected article or proposed article
- risk type
- options: approve, narrow, merge, split, defer, reject, refresh, archive
- recommended decision

## 19. Operational Defaults

When uncertain:

- narrow the cluster
- strengthen the pillar
- merge overlap
- map intent before drafting
- define the persona
- validate evidence
- link for reader clarity
- refresh before expanding
- escalate founder-level decisions

StrongPath should feel like a clear editorial system, not a pile of articles.
