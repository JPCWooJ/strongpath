# COPY_GOVERNANCE_STANDARD.md

StrongPath Copy Governance Standard

Canonical version - Phase 1

## Purpose

This file prevents internal planning language, process notes, strategic scaffolding, and agent-facing explanations from leaking into public StrongPath copy.

It governs public-facing website, article, commerce, and email copy. It applies to humans and agents writing or implementing StrongPath content.

## Root Problem

StrongPath has governance, strategy, personas, publishing plans, product plans, and implementation prompts that are useful internally but unsafe or ineffective when surfaced to readers.

The root failure is not individual wording. The root failure is treating internal explanation as public persuasion.

Public readers do not need to see how StrongPath thinks about itself. They need to understand:

- what problem is being clarified
- why the source is trustworthy
- what action is useful now
- what to read, buy, join, or ignore next

## Governing Rule

Public copy must be written for the reader's decision, not for the team's internal understanding.

Any phrase that exists primarily to explain StrongPath's roadmap, publishing status, brand architecture, product intent, authority model, or implementation logic is not public copy unless it has been rewritten into reader-facing value.

## Four-Tag System

Every copy source, note, draft, or governance excerpt used in public work should be mentally classified with one of these tags before it is reused.

### [PUBLIC_COPY_SAFE]

Definition:
Copy that can appear in public with little or no adaptation.

Allowed use:

- Website body copy
- Article headlines and body copy
- Commerce CTAs and product descriptions
- Email subject lines and body copy
- Social or paid media copy, if otherwise compliant

Rules:

- Must speak to the reader's problem, decision, or next step.
- Must avoid internal strategy terms.
- Must be clear without requiring knowledge of StrongPath operations.
- Must pass the pre-publication copy scan in this file.

Examples:

- "Find your starting point."
- "Read the muscle-loss guide."
- "Buy on Amazon."
- "No miracle claims. No supplement hype."

### [EDITORIAL_REFERENCE_ONLY]

Definition:
Material that can inform public copy but should not be copied directly without editorial transformation.

Allowed use:

- Background for articles
- Source framing for homepage or commerce copy
- Tone and positioning guidance
- Editorial briefing material

Rules:

- May shape public claims, but must be rewritten for the reader.
- Must not appear as process description.
- Must not expose content strategy, homepage architecture, or publication planning.
- Must be translated into practical reader benefit before publication.

Examples:

- Persona language about Margaret noticing stairs can become public copy about stairs, groceries, chairs, and independence.
- A governance note that the book is the credibility anchor can become "Start with the book" or "Read the deeper case for strength."

### [INTERNAL_ONLY]

Definition:
Material that may be useful for planning, implementation, governance, or agent coordination but must not appear in public copy.

Allowed use:

- Agent prompts
- Workstream planning
- Implementation notes
- Internal critiques
- Governance discussions
- Roadmap and product planning

Rules:

- Must not appear on public pages, articles, commerce modules, emails, metadata, alt text, or CTAs.
- Must not be lightly paraphrased into public copy if the public version still reads like internal explanation.
- Must be removed or rewritten into reader-facing language before publication.

Examples:

- "Now publishing"
- "The first StrongPath essays are live"
- "StrongPath will"
- "Our platform"
- "We are building"
- "Book authority"
- "Internal roadmap"
- "Phase 1 activation"

### [STRATEGIC_CONTEXT_ONLY]

Definition:
Strategic framing that defines business direction, authority hierarchy, or product intent but should not be used as public copy.

Allowed use:

- Governance interpretation
- Product and commerce planning
- Homepage architecture decisions
- Agent handoff context
- Internal prioritization

Rules:

- May determine what a page does.
- May not appear as the words a reader sees unless rewritten.
- Must be translated into reader-facing utility, trust, or action.
- Must not expose future product plans as promises.

Examples:

- "Editorial-commerce pathway" can become "Books, guides, and tools."
- "Platform-as-second-edition framing" can inform book placement, but should not become public homepage copy unless explicitly approved.
- "Commerce architecture" can become a simple reader path: book, guides, waitlist, tools.

## Prohibited Public Leakage

These phrases and patterns must not appear in public StrongPath copy unless the founder explicitly approves a specific exception:

- "The first StrongPath essays are live"
- "First essays"
- "Now publishing"
- "StrongPath will"
- "Our platform"
- "We are building"
- "Book authority"
- "Authority object"
- "Internal roadmap"
- "Phase 1"
- "Platform will"
- "Publication architecture"
- "Homepage architecture"
- "Editorial-commerce structure" as visible page copy
- "Internal explanatory narration"

Also prohibited:

- Copy that explains why a section exists instead of why the reader should use it.
- Copy that names a content strategy instead of a reader benefit.
- Copy that describes future products as if they are already available.
- Copy that uses governance terminology as public-facing language.

## Homepage Copy Rules

Homepage copy must answer four reader questions quickly:

1. What is this?
2. Who is it for?
3. Why should I trust it?
4. What should I do next?

Homepage copy must:

- Lead with reader situations, not StrongPath's publishing status.
- Make book, guide, waitlist, tools, and program paths clear.
- Use concrete reader pains: stairs, groceries, chairs, balance, recovery, helping a parent, wanting a serious plan.
- Keep commerce contextual and earned.
- Use labels that orient a reader, not labels that reveal an internal content model.

Homepage copy must not:

- Describe StrongPath as "building" something.
- Use section labels like "Book authority" or "Editorial-commerce section."
- Explain the homepage strategy in visible copy.
- Treat future tools, programs, or supplements as already live.

## Article Copy Rules

Article copy must:

- Open with a real reader problem or question.
- Clarify the mechanism, evidence, and practical implication.
- Distinguish between current third-party evidence and book-level credibility.
- Use the book as credibility context only when appropriate.
- Avoid unsupported medical claims.

Article copy must not:

- Announce publishing status.
- Refer to articles as "essays" unless there is an editorial reason approved for that article.
- Mention internal content plans, clusters, pillars, or keyword strategy.
- Use the article body to explain StrongPath's roadmap.

## Commerce Copy Rules

Commerce copy must:

- Make the product or action useful in context.
- Clarify who it is for and what decision it helps with.
- Keep the book visible as a legitimate authority and purchase path.
- State future products carefully and conditionally.
- Respect affiliate and medical disclosure requirements.

Commerce copy must not:

- Use pressure tactics.
- Create supplement hype.
- Overpromise outcomes.
- Present future tools, programs, or supplements as available before they are live.
- Use vague CTAs when a clearer action exists. Prefer "Buy on Amazon" over "View the book" when the link is a purchase path.

## Email Copy Rules

Email copy must:

- Give the reader a reason to open, read, and act.
- Use practical benefits, not launch status.
- Segment by reader situation when possible: self, parent, serious plan, clinician/evaluator.
- Preserve calm authority and restraint.

Email copy must not:

- Announce internal roadmap progress as the primary value.
- Say "we are building" unless the email is explicitly a founder/product update and approved for that purpose.
- Use "now publishing" or "first essays."
- Turn future products into implied promises.

## Codex Implementation Rules

Codex and other implementation agents must:

- Read this file before public copy edits.
- Treat governance language as context, not automatically reusable copy.
- Scan changed files for prohibited phrases before finalizing.
- Rewrite visible labels that only serve internal section logic.
- Preserve the distinction between public copy and implementation comments.
- Report any intentional exception in the final response.

Codex and other implementation agents must not:

- Copy user prompts, planning language, section architecture labels, or governance terminology directly into the UI.
- Add public copy that explains implementation decisions.
- Use internal tags as visible page text.
- Modify unrelated governance files while applying this standard unless explicitly instructed.

## Required Pre-Publication Copy Scan

Before publishing any homepage, article, commerce, or email copy, run a copy scan for prohibited leakage.

At minimum, search for:

- "first essays"
- "now publishing"
- "StrongPath will"
- "our platform"
- "we are building"
- "Book authority"
- "authority object"
- "internal roadmap"
- "Phase 1"
- "platform will"
- "editorial-commerce section"
- "publication architecture"
- "homepage architecture"

If any term appears in public-facing copy:

1. Remove it.
2. Rewrite it into reader-facing value.
3. Re-scan.
4. Document any approved exception.

Suggested command:

```bash
rg -i "first essays|now publishing|StrongPath will|our platform|we are building|Book authority|authority object|internal roadmap|Phase 1|platform will|editorial-commerce section|publication architecture|homepage architecture" app docs --glob "!docs/governance/COPY_GOVERNANCE_STANDARD.md"
```

## Escalation Rules

Escalate to the founder before publication when:

- A phrase from [INTERNAL_ONLY] is proposed for public use.
- Strategic roadmap language seems necessary to explain a page.
- Future product copy could imply availability, efficacy, or a launch commitment.
- A commerce claim could read as medical, supplement, or outcome promise.
- A copy choice conflicts with BRAND.md, PERSONAS.md, or this standard.
- A public page needs to quote founder language, internal persona language, or governance language directly.

When in doubt, rewrite toward the reader's practical decision.
