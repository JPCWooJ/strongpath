# COPY_GOVERNANCE_STANDARD.md

## Purpose

Canonical StrongPath copy governance standard.

This file defines required copy posture for public-facing StrongPath writing: brand voice, persona fit, claim discipline, citation awareness, CTA restraint, commerce separation, prohibited language, and final copy review.

## 1. Core Principle

StrongPath copy is serious, direct, and hopeful, in that order.

Copy must sound like a specialist, not a salesman. It should help an intelligent reader understand the problem, trust the evidence, and take the next appropriate step without fear, hype, or pressure.

## 2. Required Copy Posture

StrongPath copy must be:

- evidence-aware
- underpromising
- specific
- calm
- practical
- respectful
- clear about limits
- free of filler

Do not use urgency, shame, fear, or transformation promises to create action.

## 3. Persona Fit

Every piece of copy must identify the primary reader.

Rules:

- Margaret-facing copy is peer-level, specific, and never patronizing.
- David-facing copy is goodwill-led, practical, and not fear-led.
- Dr. Chen-facing copy is citation-visible, clinically respectful, and not consumer-urgent.

Do not blend personas into generic wellness copy.

## 4. Claim Discipline

Copy must not make a claim stronger than the evidence.

Use cautious verbs when appropriate:

- may help
- can support
- is associated with
- research suggests
- evidence supports
- can improve
- helps explain

Avoid medical, guaranteed, or outcome-promise wording unless directly quoted from a source and clearly contextualized.

## 5. Prohibited Language

Do not use StrongPath voice to claim:

- cure
- reverse
- prevent disease
- guarantee
- transform
- hack
- secret
- miracle
- anti-aging
- breakthrough

The word "reversing" appears only inside the italicized book title *Choosing the StrongPath: Reversing the Downward Spiral of Aging*.

## 6. Citation And Evidence Awareness

Copy must preserve the source hierarchy in `EDITORIAL_STANDARDS.md`.

Rules:

- current third-party sources carry health claims
- the book is credibility, not substantiation
- citation links and commerce links remain separate
- quantitative claims require a source
- safety, intervention, or disease-adjacent claims require stronger support

If the evidence is not available, narrow the claim or cut it.

## 7. CTA Discipline

CTAs must match reader readiness.

Rules:

- education comes before commerce
- one primary CTA per asset
- secondary CTA may support book or related education
- CTAs must not imply diagnosis, treatment, prevention, or guaranteed outcome
- quiz/program/product CTAs must stay narrower than research claims

Good CTA posture: next useful step.

Bad CTA posture: pressure, urgency, fear, or outcome promise.

## 8. Commerce Separation

Copy must preserve the book/product firewall.

Rules:

- affiliate or product links support buying action, not evidence
- product copy describes format, use, audience, or educational purpose
- product copy does not inherit research claims
- book copy supports credibility and origin, not current health substantiation

If commerce language depends on research authority to close the sale, revise.

## 9. SEO Copy Rules

Search visibility must not distort copy.

Rules:

- answer search intent early
- use natural keyword language
- avoid keyword stuffing
- avoid unsafe exact-match phrasing
- metadata must not overclaim beyond the article body
- headings must clarify, not perform SEO

Search demand shapes the doorway. Evidence and brand govern the room.

## 10. Final Copy Review Checklist

Before publication, confirm:

- primary persona is clear
- copy sounds serious, direct, hopeful
- no prohibited language appears outside allowed context
- claims are evidence-safe
- citations support substantive claims
- book is not used as proof for health claims
- internal links are honest and useful
- CTA is appropriate to reader readiness
- commerce and evidence are separated
- medical/legal caveats appear when needed

Failure on any item means revise before publication.
