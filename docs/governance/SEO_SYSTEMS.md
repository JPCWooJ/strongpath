# SEO_SYSTEMS.md

## Purpose

Canonical Layer 2 SEO governance and search-visibility system for StrongPath.

This file defines how StrongPath uses search demand without compromising editorial trust, citation discipline, brand posture, claim safety, or the book/product firewall.

This is an editorial governance document. It does not define analytics/reporting systems, backlink outreach systems, programmatic SEO systems, AI-content-farm workflows, publishing calendars, or CMS implementation architecture.

## 1. Core Principle

StrongPath SEO is trust-first.

Search visibility exists to help the right reader find credible, evidence-based strength guidance. It does not authorize keyword stuffing, unsafe claim language, thin content, content farms, programmatic sprawl, or commerce-first pages disguised as education.

The rule:

> Search demand shapes the doorway. Evidence and brand govern the room.

## 2. Pillar/Satellite Architecture

StrongPath uses a focused pillar/satellite model.

Pillars:

- define major topic territory
- carry broad search intent
- establish authority for the cluster
- link to supporting satellites
- cite deeply
- answer the reader's core question without forcing a commercial next step

Satellites:

- answer narrower questions
- serve long-tail or persona-specific intent
- link back to the relevant pillar
- deepen mechanisms, objections, safety, or execution
- avoid duplicating pillar scope

No article should exist without a clear role: pillar, satellite, book/authority page, product/support page, FAQ, or tool/support asset.

## 3. Topical Clustering Rules

Clusters must map to StrongPath's editorial pillars and persona needs.

Canonical cluster families:

- sarcopenia and age-related muscle loss
- strength as independence
- resistance training execution
- protein, recovery, and progression
- caregiver support and parent-strength guidance
- book and brand-term defense
- safety, late-start, and older-adult adaptation

Rules:

- one primary cluster per article
- one primary search intent per article
- one primary persona or persona-moment per article
- secondary keywords support the topic; they do not redirect it
- overlapping drafts are merged, narrowed, or assigned different intents

If two articles would answer the same searcher in the same way, create one better article.

## 4. Internal Linking Governance

Internal links teach readers and search engines how StrongPath thinks.

Rules:

- every satellite links to its parent pillar
- every pillar links to the most useful satellites
- internal links must be editorially useful, not decorative
- anchor text should describe the destination honestly
- anchor text must not overclaim
- commerce links and evidence links must remain distinct
- book links support credibility and reader depth, not health-claim proof
- no page should be a dead end when a relevant next step exists

Priority link targets:

- the sarcopenia pillar for age-related muscle loss context
- the resistance-training pillar for execution guidance
- the protein pillar for nutrition support
- the caregiver pillar for David-facing parent guidance
- the book page for category credibility

Do not create internal links only to manipulate ranking.

## 5. Metadata Standards

Metadata should be accurate, restrained, and search-aligned.

Title tags:

- include the primary keyword or close natural variant
- match the article's actual answer
- avoid hype, fear, and overclaiming
- do not use forbidden brand language

Meta descriptions:

- state the reader problem clearly
- reflect the evidence-safe angle
- include a useful promise, not a medical promise
- avoid stronger claims than the article body supports

Open graph/social metadata:

- should preserve the same claim discipline as search metadata
- must not use clickbait versions of the article claim

Metadata is copy. It must pass `CONTENT_QA.md`.

## 6. URL Structure Rules

URLs should be stable, short, readable, and keyword-bearing.

Rules:

- use lowercase words separated by hyphens
- keep slugs permanent after publication
- match the article's durable topic, not a temporary headline
- avoid dates unless the date is core to the asset
- avoid hype words, medical promises, and unnecessary modifiers
- do not create multiple URLs for the same search intent

Preferred pattern:

```txt
/blog/what-is-sarcopenia
/blog/resistance-training-older-adults
/blog/protein-for-older-adults
/book
```

Changing a URL is a governance decision because it risks accumulated authority.

## 7. Search-Intent Alignment

Every article must identify search intent before drafting.

Intent types:

- informational: reader wants explanation
- executional: reader wants what to do
- safety: reader wants to know if something is safe
- caregiver: reader is acting for a parent
- commercial-adjacent: reader may buy but still needs education
- navigational: reader is looking for StrongPath or the book

Rules:

- answer the intent early
- do not turn informational intent into a sales page
- do not bury safety guidance
- do not target crisis queries outside scope
- do not target clinician queries as Phase 1 consumer content

Intent mismatch is an SEO and trust failure.

## 8. Keyword Usage Discipline

`keyword-universe.md` is a map, not permission to use every phrase in public copy.

Rules:

- use persona language over awkward exact-match phrasing
- use primary keywords naturally in title, opener, headings, or metadata when safe
- use secondary keywords only where they serve the reader
- do not stuff keywords
- do not let "senior" or "elderly" violate persona rules
- do not echo unsafe "reverse," "cure," "anti-aging," or "hack" phrasing
- excluded keywords remain excluded unless governance changes

Verbatim persona phrases are trust signals. Use them where they match the reader's actual thought.

## 9. Search-Safe Claim Rules

SEO claims must follow `CITATION_STANDARD.md`, `RESEARCH_SYSTEMS.md`, and `CONTENT_QA.md`.

Rules:

- "prevent" queries require education-safe framing
- "reverse" queries are rewritten into evidence-safe language
- disease-adjacent claims require citations and hedging
- fall-risk and frailty topics require medical/legal caution
- supplement topics require commerce-sensitive handling
- metadata cannot make claims stronger than the article body
- headings cannot overstate evidence for ranking
- snippets should invite trust, not fear

If the only way to rank for a keyword is to violate claim discipline, do not target the keyword.

## 10. Article Hierarchy Rules

Article hierarchy should make the answer easy to scan.

Rules:

- H1 states the topic plainly
- opener answers why the article matters
- early section defines the problem or question
- mechanism section explains why the answer works
- evidence appears before strong recommendations
- safety limits appear before action steps when risk is present
- commerce or product mentions come after education
- conclusion routes to one useful next step

Headings should carry search intent, reader language, and editorial clarity. They should not become keyword lists.

## 11. Content Freshness And Updates

StrongPath's platform-as-second-edition framing depends on current, maintained content.

Update review is required when:

- a pillar article relies on sources more than five years old
- a safety, supplement, fall-risk, frailty, or post-event topic may have changed
- a source URL breaks or changes materially
- article claims no longer match current research
- brand, citation, research, or QA governance changes
- a newer article creates overlap or a better internal link path

Updates should improve accuracy, clarity, and trust. Do not refresh content only to signal freshness.

## 12. Authority-Building Strategy

StrongPath authority compounds through:

- strong pillar pages
- useful satellites
- visible citation discipline
- clear internal linking
- consistent book credibility framing
- current research
- persona-accurate language
- underpromising claims
- high-quality answers to narrow questions

Authority is not built by publishing volume alone.

Every article should strengthen at least one of:

- topical authority
- reader trust
- citation record
- internal link graph
- book/brand defensibility
- safe commercial pathway

## 13. Discovery Pathway Logic

Searchers should move from question to clarity to next action.

Common pathways:

- problem awareness: sarcopenia or muscle loss article to execution article
- execution: resistance training or protein article to starter protocol
- caregiver: parent-strength article to safety or older-adult evidence article
- book discovery: book page to topic pillar or quiz
- commercial-adjacent: product or affiliate topic to education first, purchase second

Rules:

- the first page must satisfy the search intent
- next steps must be relevant to that intent
- commercial next steps must not interrupt evidence trust
- caregiver paths should reduce overwhelm
- Margaret paths should preserve peer-level respect
- clinician-visible paths should surface citation discipline

## 14. Affiliate SEO Restrictions

Affiliate SEO is allowed only when it does not weaken trust.

Rules:

- education comes before purchase recommendation
- affiliate pages must disclose where required
- affiliate links do not serve as evidence links
- product comparisons must not imply clinical outcomes
- supplement content must avoid disease, treatment, prevention, or reversal claims
- product pages cannot rank by borrowing unsupported health claims
- review-style articles require commerce-sensitive QA

Do not let StrongPath read as a supplement site pretending to be an education platform.

## 15. AI-Search And Discoverability Considerations

AI search rewards clear, structured, trustworthy answers.

StrongPath should:

- answer the main question plainly
- use consistent terminology
- define technical terms in reader language
- cite named sources
- separate claims from product language
- maintain clear article hierarchy
- keep authorial and brand authority accurate
- avoid generic filler that adds no information

Do not create AI-targeted content farms, spun variants, or near-duplicate pages for discoverability.

The same trust-first article should work for human readers, search engines, and AI answer systems.

## 16. Escalation Rules

Escalate before publishing or targeting when:

- keyword opportunity requires unsafe claim language
- search intent is crisis, clinical, or outside Phase 1 scope
- article overlaps an existing page
- URL change is proposed
- metadata would need to overclaim to compete
- affiliate opportunity pressures the book/product firewall
- supplement topic creates FTC/FDA risk
- internal anchor text implies unsupported outcomes
- AI-search tactic would create thin, duplicate, or low-trust content
- brand-term strategy conflicts with current author-presence rules

Escalation output must state:

- search opportunity
- risk
- affected page or proposed page
- recommended decision: target, narrow, defer, merge, rewrite, or reject

## 17. Operational Defaults

When uncertain:

- serve the reader first
- preserve claim discipline
- choose the narrower keyword
- merge overlap
- link where useful
- keep metadata honest
- separate evidence from commerce
- defer unsafe opportunities
- escalate before creating authority drift

StrongPath SEO wins by being more credible than louder competitors.
