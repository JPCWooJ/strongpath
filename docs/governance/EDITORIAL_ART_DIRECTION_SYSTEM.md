# Editorial Art Direction System

Purpose: define StrongPath's reusable rules for premium article presentation. This system governs visual hierarchy, editorial energy, art direction, and mobile readability for flagship and satellite articles.

Governed by:
- `docs/governance/BRAND.md`
- `docs/governance/DESIGN.md`

## Core Principle

StrongPath article design must make evidence feel alive without making it feel sold.

The article experience should be calm, specific, visually memorable, and easy to read. It should never feel like a generic wellness blog, a supplement funnel, a SaaS landing page, or an academic PDF.

## Hero Design

Flagship pillars require a distinct hero system:
- category, date, and reading time
- strong headline with controlled wrapping
- concise deck that answers search intent without keyword stuffing
- one dominant visual that expresses the article's central idea
- clear mobile stacking order: metadata, title, deck, visual

Hero visuals must carry meaning. For example:
- sarcopenia: strength, muscle, function
- walking: aerobic value versus strength demand
- protein: meal distribution and muscle repair
- caregiver guidance: support without pressure
- late-start training: progression and confidence

Avoid:
- decorative gradient-only heroes
- generic stock seniors
- fear-based medical imagery
- oversized type that creates awkward mobile wraps
- visual systems that imply medical certainty beyond the article

## Article Visuals

Every flagship article should include 2-4 editorial modules. Satellite articles may use 1-2 modules.

Approved module types:
- key takeaways
- evidence note
- practical test
- stat module
- mechanism visual
- source record
- read-next pathway
- clinician/safety note

Modules must have different jobs. Do not repeat the same point in multiple visual treatments.

## Section Rhythm

Article pages should alternate between:
- readable body text
- section heading
- short list or question sequence
- evidence module
- practical example
- read-next or CTA module

Do not allow more than roughly 500-700 words of uninterrupted body text in a flagship article.

Section headings should be direct and editorial. Avoid clever headings that obscure search intent.

## Evidence Callouts

Evidence callouts exist to clarify claim strength.

Each evidence callout should include:
- what the evidence says
- why it matters
- citation or source proximity where appropriate
- restrained language

Evidence callouts must not:
- exaggerate findings
- turn correlations into promises
- use unsupported percentages
- make product claims
- replace citations

## Stat Modules

Use stat modules sparingly.

Approved uses:
- public-health guidance
- consensus recommendations
- clearly sourced prevalence or risk figures
- simple comparison points

Rules:
- every stat must be tied to a source
- the number must be visually readable on mobile
- the label must explain the context
- no stat should be used only for drama

## Image Selection

Preferred image/art directions:
- commissioned-feeling editorial illustration
- concrete functional scenarios
- mechanism diagrams
- documentary-style human context when dignity-preserving
- object/detail imagery where inspection matters

Prohibited or discouraged:
- generic happy senior stock photos
- before/after body imagery
- medical fear imagery
- supplement/product glamour shots inside evidence sections
- copyrighted editorial images from reference publications

## Typography Scale

Typography should create hierarchy without overwhelming the reader.

Rules:
- hero headlines may be large, but must wrap cleanly on mobile
- body copy should remain comfortable for long reading
- module headings should be smaller than hero headings
- stat numerals may be large only when labels remain close and legible
- no viewport-width font scaling
- no negative letter spacing

## Color And Contrast

StrongPath's article palette should use:
- inkwell for authority and contrast
- parchment for reading surfaces
- verdigris for calm structure
- sunbeam as a controlled accent
- verdigris wash for soft evidence surfaces

Use darker editorial bands when the article needs energy, but preserve readability.

Avoid:
- beige-only pages
- purple-blue wellness gradients
- decorative orbs
- low-contrast text
- color used without hierarchy

## Mobile Article Design

Mobile is the baseline article experience.

Required:
- hero title must not crowd the visual
- metadata must wrap cleanly
- hero visual must remain legible at narrow widths
- modules must stack with clear borders and spacing
- buttons and links must not collide with body text
- source records must remain readable

If a visual only works on desktop, it is not approved.

## CTA Treatment

Article CTAs should be quiet and pathway-based.

Hierarchy:
1. related article or cluster pathway
2. book page when contextually relevant
3. waitlist/email only when useful and non-disruptive

CTAs must not interrupt evidence sections or appear to commercialize medical claims.

## Review Checklist

Before publication or redesign approval, verify:
- hero has a clear visual thesis
- visual modules support the article, not decoration
- body text is not visually monotonous
- mobile hierarchy is clean
- evidence and stat modules are sourced or source-adjacent
- CTA placement respects the commerce firewall
- no visual claims exceed article claims
- no copyrighted reference imagery is used

## Escalation

Escalate to founder review when:
- a visual implies diagnosis, prevention, reversal, or treatment
- a module uses a statistic not already source-validated
- imagery may be fear-based or dignity-reducing
- commerce, product, or affiliate placement appears near medical claims
- a design pattern departs from StrongPath brand or design governance
