# Content Brief Standard

## Purpose

- Define the minimum brief required before drafting StrongPath content.
- Remove guessing for writers, reviewers, and Codex.
- Keep claims, evidence, links, CTAs, and risks explicit.

## Core Rules

- A brief is a drafting contract.
- Keep briefs short, specific, and operational.
- Governance beats the brief.
- A brief may narrow claims; it may not expand claim authority.
- Do not draft when persona, intent, claims, evidence, or risk is unclear.

## Required Fields

- Working title.
- Content type.
- Primary persona and moment.
- Reader question.
- Search or editorial intent.
- Article role: pillar, satellite, FAQ, book/authority, product/support, refresh.
- Target keyword or phrase, if relevant.
- Cluster and parent/sibling relationship.
- Approved claims.
- Do-not-claim list.
- Evidence requirements or research-packet pointer.
- Internal-link plan.
- CTA direction.
- Commerce boundaries.
- Tone and language constraints.
- Risk and escalation flags.
- Founder questions, if any.
- Approval state and next owner.

## Content Types

- Pillar: broad authority page; requires deeper research and likely founder gate.
- Satellite: narrow support article; links to parent pillar.
- FAQ: short answer; source substantive claims.
- Book/authority page: credibility and origin; no book-as-clinical-proof.
- Product/support page: commerce-sensitive; product claims stay narrow.
- Refresh/update: names what changed and what must be reverified.

## Approved Claims

Each approved claim needs:

- wording or boundary
- source or packet reference
- citation class
- required hedge
- article citation requirement

If the source does not support the wording, rewrite the claim before drafting.

## Do-Not-Claim

List prohibited territory:

- diagnosis, treatment, prevention, cure, reversal
- guaranteed outcomes
- unsupported mechanisms
- fear-based urgency
- product or supplement efficacy beyond evidence
- book-as-clinical-proof language
- clinician-level advice without authority
- unsafe fall, frailty, surgery, hospitalization, or chronic-condition claims

## Evidence Requirements

- State whether a research packet is required.
- Name source classes needed.
- Name claims requiring visible citations.
- Name freshness expectations.
- Name safety or disclaimer requirements.
- Do not hand off raw links without extraction notes.

## Links And CTA

- Identify required pillar and satellite links.
- Keep evidence links, internal links, and commerce links distinct.
- Anchor text must be honest and claim-safe.
- CTA must match reader intent.
- Education comes before commerce.
- CTA must not imply diagnosis, treatment, prevention, reversal, or guaranteed outcome.

## Brief States

- Draft Brief: incomplete direction.
- Research Needed: evidence or claim boundary missing.
- Escalation Needed: risk blocks approval.
- Revision Needed: direction exists but needs correction.
- Approved Brief: drafting may begin.

## Pass / Fail

Pass when a writer can answer:

- what am I writing?
- who is it for?
- what intent does it serve?
- what can I claim?
- what must I avoid?
- what evidence must I use?
- what links and CTAs belong?
- what risks need care?

Fail when:

- writer must invent core direction
- claims or do-not-claims are vague
- evidence is missing for substantive claims
- persona or intent is generic
- commerce boundaries are unclear
- founder question is not decision-ready

## Escalate

- Medical/legal risk.
- Evidence uncertainty.
- Commerce or affiliate sensitivity.
- SEO pressure toward unsafe wording.
- Strategic positioning or pillar direction.
- Book, founder, author, product, quiz, or platform framing.
- Governance conflict.
