# Citation Standard

## Purpose

- Define what evidence can support StrongPath claims.
- Keep health, safety, commerce, book, and affiliate claims disciplined.
- Make citations useful to writers, reviewers, Codex, and readers.

## Core Rules

- Specific health claims need current, named, third-party support.
- The book is credibility, not substantiation for current health claims.
- Cite mechanisms, guidance, quantitative claims, interventions, risks, outcomes, and safety limits.
- If a claim cannot be supported cleanly, narrow it or cut it.
- Search demand never authorizes unsafe claim language.
- Affiliate and product links are not evidence links.
- Evidence links stay close to the claims they support.

## Source Hierarchy

- Tier 1: current peer-reviewed research, reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines.
- Tier 2: named institutions: NIH, NIA, CDC, Mayo Clinic, Harvard Health, ACSM, academic medical centers.
- Tier 3: landmark older studies, labeled as older and paired with current sources when practice claims are made.
- Tier 4: `Choosing the StrongPath`, used only for credibility, origin, book page, or history.
- Reject: AI answers, unsourced blogs, influencer posts, supplement-brand claims, retailer pages, testimonials, forums, competitor marketing, Amazon reviews.

## Claims Requiring Citation

- Quantitative claims.
- Medical, clinical, physiological, safety, or mechanism claims.
- Claims about research findings, prevalence, risk, recovery, outcomes, treatment, prevention, reversal, diagnosis, supplements, nutrition, protein, resistance training, fall risk, frailty, surgery, hospitalization, or chronic conditions.
- Claims used near product, quiz, program, affiliate, or purchase recommendations.

## Claim Language

- Prefer: may help, can support, evidence suggests, is associated with, is linked to, contributes to, helps explain.
- Avoid: cure, treat, reverse, prevent disease, guarantee, transform, eliminate, restore as a promise, fix.
- Use "reversing" only inside the full book title.
- Underpromise by default.

## Book Standard

- First mention: `Choosing the StrongPath: Reversing the Downward Spiral of Aging`.
- Subsequent mentions: `Choosing the StrongPath`.
- The book may support origin, authority, and mission.
- The book may not support current science, safety, product, supplement, outcome, or medical claims.
- Author names appear only where `BRAND.md` permits.

## Commerce Firewall

- Research claims do not transfer to products.
- Product claims describe format, contents, audience, educational purpose, or user experience.
- Product claims must not imply diagnosis, treatment, prevention, reversal, medical recovery, physical therapy equivalence, or guaranteed outcomes.
- Affiliate content requires disclosure when applicable.
- Amazon links use the approved Associates tag when present.
- Keep purchase links away from evidence sentences.

## Article Reference Standard

- Pillar articles need multiple strong sources and a visible source list.
- Satellite articles need enough sources for every substantive claim and safety boundary.
- FAQs need one strong source per substantive answer unless purely navigational.
- Product pages cite only educational claims; product descriptions stay narrow.
- Emails and social posts route specific claims to cited articles when possible.

## Freshness

- Use current sources for current practice, safety, supplements, resistance training, protein, fall risk, frailty, post-event recovery, and clinician-visible claims.
- Review sources older than five years when they support current practice or high-risk claims.
- Older landmark studies are acceptable when named as older and not used as current guidance by themselves.

## Pass / Fail

Pass when:

- every substantive claim has appropriate support
- citation is close enough to map to the claim
- source quality matches claim risk
- the book is not used as health-claim proof
- evidence, commerce, and affiliate links are separated
- language is hedged to the source

Fail when:

- a health or quantitative claim lacks support
- source does not support the exact claim
- citation is stale for a current-practice claim
- product or affiliate language borrows research authority
- forbidden promise language appears

## Escalate

- Medical, legal, FTC, FDA, affiliate, supplement, fall-risk, frailty, post-surgery, hospitalization, or contraindication risk.
- Conflicting sources.
- Weak or stale source is the only support.
- Clinician-visible claim lacks clinical-grade evidence.
- Book, author, founder, product, quiz, or program framing is outside current governance.
- Evidence and commerce cannot be cleanly separated.
