# Research Systems

## Purpose

- Define how StrongPath research becomes safe article claims.
- Keep research packets short, reusable, and useful to writers and reviewers.
- Prevent raw-link handoffs and unsupported claims.

## Core Rules

- Research exists to define what StrongPath can responsibly say.
- Do not collect sources for volume.
- Current consensus beats isolated findings.
- The book is credibility, not substantiation.
- Commerce-sensitive topics need evidence boundaries before product language.
- No raw links without extraction notes.

## Source Order

- Current reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, or consensus sources.
- Current primary studies when needed.
- Named institutional sources.
- Landmark older studies for history or durable findings.
- Book or founder-history references only for credibility context.

Reject AI answers, unsourced blogs, influencer posts, supplement-brand claims, retailer pages, testimonials, forums, competitor marketing, and the book as proof for current health claims.

## Research Workflow

- Define research question.
- Define reader/persona and claim territory.
- Identify risk level and citation classes.
- Source candidate materials.
- Screen sources.
- Extract findings, limits, and supported claims.
- Validate claims against sources.
- Build article research packet.
- Escalate unresolved risk.

## Source Screen

Minimum fields:

- title
- author, institution, or publisher
- year or updated date
- URL or DOI
- source type
- citation class
- claim territory
- freshness: current, landmark, stale, uncertain
- risk: low, moderate, high
- decision: use, reserve, reject, escalate

## Evidence Extraction

For each approved source, extract:

- useful finding
- supported claim
- unsupported claim
- population or context
- intervention/exposure, if relevant
- outcome, if relevant
- limitations
- safety notes
- mechanism language
- recommended article use

Extract limits as carefully as findings.

## Claim Validation

Statuses:

- Approved: source supports claim as written.
- Approved with hedge: usable only with narrower language.
- Needs stronger source: plausible but insufficient.
- Rewrite: direction works, wording does not.
- Cut: unsupported or unsafe.
- Escalate: governance, medical, legal, brand, or commerce risk.

If the source does not support the exact claim, the claim is not validated.

## Research Packet

Minimum packet:

- article/topic
- reader/persona
- content type
- risk level
- evidence summary
- approved claims with sources and hedges
- do-not-claim list
- source list
- mechanisms to explain
- safety/disclaimer notes
- book/credibility use
- commerce boundaries
- writer handoff

## Freshness

- Use current sources for clinical, safety, supplement, commerce-sensitive, clinician-visible, current-practice, post-fall, post-surgery, hospitalization, frailty, contraindication, or disease-adjacent claims.
- Review sources older than five years when they support current guidance.
- Keep landmark older studies only when labeled and not used alone for present-day practice.

## Storage

- Use stable names.
- Tag by topic, persona, content type, citation class, risk, freshness, source type, slug, and commerce sensitivity.
- Do not create duplicate authority files.
- Governance stays in GitHub.

## Pass / Fail

Pass when:

- packet gives the writer safe claims and limits
- every approved claim has support
- unsupported claims are named
- source freshness and risk are clear
- commerce and book boundaries are explicit

Fail when:

- handoff is raw links
- claim boundaries are unclear
- source quality is weak or unverified
- limitations are omitted
- product or book claims inherit research authority

## Escalate

- Sources conflict.
- Claim creates medical, legal, FTC, FDA, supplement, affiliate, or commerce risk.
- Source is stale with no replacement.
- Clinician-visible claim lacks clinical-grade support.
- Topic involves fall, surgery, hospitalization, frailty, contraindications, or disease-adjacent language.
- Claim cannot be validated without over-narrowing.
