# Editorial Standards

## Purpose

- Single active standard for StrongPath editorial quality.
- Govern citations, claims, briefs, QA, scoring, research, SEO, links, article presentation, refresh, and escalation.
- Keep rules short, observable, and shippable.

## Citation And Sources

- Specific health, safety, mechanism, intervention, nutrition, quantitative, or outcome claims need current, named, third-party support.
- Best sources: peer-reviewed research, reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, NIH/NIA/CDC/Mayo/Harvard Health/ACSM, and academic medical centers.
- Older landmark studies are acceptable only when labeled as older and not used alone for current practice.
- Reject as evidence: AI answers, unsourced blogs, influencer posts, supplement-brand claims, retailer pages, testimonials, forums, competitor marketing, Amazon reviews.
- The book is credibility, not substantiation for current health claims.
- Evidence links stay close to the claims they support.
- Affiliate and product links are not evidence links.

## Claims And Hedging

- Cite or cut health, safety, mechanism, intervention, supplement, protein, resistance-training, fall-risk, frailty, surgery, hospitalization, chronic-condition, and quantitative claims.
- Preferred language: may help, can support, evidence suggests, is associated with, is linked to, contributes to, helps explain.
- Avoid: cure, treat, reverse, prevent disease, guarantee, transform, eliminate, restore as a promise, fix.
- Use "reversing" only inside the full book title.
- Product claims describe format, contents, audience, educational purpose, or user experience.
- Product claims must not imply diagnosis, treatment, prevention, reversal, medical recovery, physical therapy equivalence, or guaranteed outcomes.

## Brief Minimums

- Working title.
- Content type and article role.
- Primary persona and reader moment.
- Reader question or search/editorial intent.
- Approved claims.
- Do-not-claim list.
- Evidence requirements or research-packet pointer.
- Internal-link plan.
- CTA direction.
- Commerce boundaries.
- Tone and language constraints.
- Risk flags and founder questions, if any.

## Research Packet

- Do not hand off raw links.
- Packet must include:
  - topic and reader
  - risk level
  - evidence summary
  - approved claims with sources and hedges
  - unsupported claims
  - source list
  - safety notes
  - book/credibility limits
  - commerce boundaries
  - writer handoff
- If the source does not support the exact claim, the claim is not validated.

## QA Checklist

- Article answers reader intent early.
- Claims are supported, hedged, or removed.
- Citations resolve and map to claims.
- Persona fit passes.
- Tone is serious, direct, hopeful.
- Metadata and headings do not overclaim.
- Internal links are relevant and claim-safe.
- Evidence, internal, book, and commerce links are separated.
- Commerce firewall passes.
- Medical/legal escalation triggers are resolved.
- Article presentation is readable on mobile.
- Build/typecheck pass when implementation is involved.

## Scoring And Pass/Fail

- Score only after basic QA.
- Publish ready requires:
  - total score 85+
  - no hard stops
  - evidence 12/15+
  - citation 12/15+
  - commerce firewall 8/10+
  - required founder gate resolved
  - all escalations resolved
- Pillar articles should normally score 90+.
- Score bands:
  - 90-100: strong publish candidate
  - 85-89: publish candidate after minor fixes or gate
  - 75-84: targeted revision
  - 60-74: major revision or research
  - below 60: rebuild, defer, or rewrite brief

## SEO Safety

- Search demand shapes structure; evidence and brand govern claims.
- One article needs one primary intent.
- Metadata cannot claim more than the page body.
- Do not target keywords that require unsafe language.
- Use persona language over awkward exact match.
- Do not create thin pages, duplicate pages, keyword variants, or content farms.
- URL slugs should be short, stable, lowercase, hyphenated, and claim-safe.

## Internal Linking

- Links are reader guidance first, SEO support second.
- Every important page needs an incoming and outgoing path.
- Satellites link to parent pillars.
- Pillars link only to useful satellites.
- Anchor text must be honest, specific, and claim-safe.
- Book links support credibility, not proof.
- Use one primary CTA per article.
- CTA hierarchy: education, assessment if relevant, book, product/program, affiliate purchase.

## Article Presentation Baseline

- Use Tailwind Typography for prose rhythm.
- Use Tailwind Next.js Starter Blog patterns for article/listing structure.
- Use Ghost and Astro Paper only as structural references.
- No custom SVG, abstract art systems, or synthetic diagrams unless explicitly approved.
- Prefer professional editorial imagery or clean image treatment.
- Keep body columns narrow and readable.
- Keep mobile as baseline.
- Use modules only when useful: takeaways, evidence/source box, checklist, safety note, related reading, contextual CTA.
- Use navy, verdigris, and restrained gold for hierarchy; text contrast wins.

## Refresh And Deprecation

- Refresh when sources age out, links break, research changes, governance changes, article overlaps a stronger page, metadata/CTA overclaims, or internal links need rerouting.
- Pillars: light review every six months; full claim/citation review annually.
- Strategic, safety, supplement, commerce-sensitive, or high-traffic satellites: review every six months.
- Ordinary satellites: review annually.
- Merge pages that answer the same reader, intent, and claim territory.
- Replace pages when the reader need remains but the page is too weak to patch.
- Deprecate/archive pages that are inaccurate, unsupported, out of scope, duplicative, or risky.
- Do not leave deprecated pages in active reader pathways.

## Hard Stops

- Unsupported major health claim.
- Book used as proof for current health claim.
- Product language implies diagnosis, treatment, prevention, reversal, clinical outcome, or guarantee.
- Affiliate link used as evidence.
- Metadata or heading overclaims body evidence.
- "Reverse" or "reversing" appears outside the book title as a StrongPath claim.
- Unresolved medical/legal/commerce escalation.
- Founder asked to find routine QA defects.

## Escalate

- Medical, legal, FTC, FDA, supplement, affiliate, fall-risk, frailty, post-fall, post-surgery, hospitalization, contraindication, or disease-adjacent risk.
- Conflicting, stale, or insufficient evidence.
- Clinician-visible claim without clinical-grade support.
- Unsafe keyword opportunity.
- Commerce pressure near health claims.
- Founder, book, author, product, quiz, program, or platform framing changes.
- Pillar positioning, merge, deprecation, or URL change affecting authority.
- Reviewer disagreement on risk.
