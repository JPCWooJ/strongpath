# CITATION_STANDARD.md

## Purpose

Canonical Layer 2 editorial standard for StrongPath citations, evidence quality, claim substantiation, source hierarchy, book/product firewall, and article-level reference handling.

This file exists to make StrongPath's evidence discipline operational. It tells writers, editors, agents, and reviewers what counts as support for a claim, where sources belong, how the book may be used, and when a claim must be rewritten or cut.

This is an editorial systems document. It does not implement CMS behavior, SEO architecture, or content QA workflows.

## 1. Core Principle

StrongPath is an evidence-based strength platform.

That means:

- specific health claims require current, named, third-party support
- the book establishes credibility, not claim substantiation
- citations are part of the brand, not decorative footnotes
- the reader should be able to tell, quickly, why the claim is credible
- if a claim cannot be supported cleanly, it is rewritten or cut

The standard is simple:

> StrongPath cites mechanisms, not miracles.

## 2. Two-Layer Authority Model

StrongPath uses two distinct forms of authority.

### Layer 1: Credibility Authority

Credibility authority establishes why StrongPath has the right to speak in the category.

Credibility authority includes:

- *Choosing the StrongPath: Reversing the Downward Spiral of Aging* as the category-defining book
- the book's Amazon bestseller status in Aging, Weight Training, Exercise, and Longevity
- the platform-as-second-edition framing
- the brand's accumulating citation record
- Jeff Camp as founder and operator, not clinician or scientist

Credibility authority earns attention. It does not prove individual health claims.

### Layer 2: Substantiation Authority

Substantiation authority proves the specific claims in an article, page, email, ad, or product surface.

Substantiation authority includes:

- peer-reviewed research
- named institutional sources such as Harvard Health Publishing, Mayo Clinic, NIH, NIA, academic institutions, and comparable clinical or public-health institutions
- credible clinical guidance where appropriate
- landmark studies when they remain the standard reference
- mechanisms that can be traced to credible sources

Substantiation authority carries claims. The book does not.

## 3. Source Hierarchy

Use the strongest available source for the claim being made.

### Tier 1: Current Peer-Reviewed Research

Use for:

- specific claims about sarcopenia, dynapenia, resistance training, protein intake, recovery, adaptation, fall risk, strength gain, muscle mass, or functional outcomes
- quantitative claims
- intervention claims
- clinical or near-clinical explanations

Preferred citation information:

- author or research group
- year
- journal
- finding stated conservatively
- link or DOI where available

### Tier 2: Named Institutional Sources

Use for:

- patient-facing explanations
- definitions
- safety guidance
- public-health context
- claims that do not require primary-study detail

Acceptable examples:

- Harvard Health Publishing
- Mayo Clinic
- NIH
- NIA
- ACSM or comparable professional bodies
- university medical centers or academic institutions with clear editorial standards

### Tier 3: Landmark Older Studies

Use when:

- the study remains a recognized reference point
- the article explains why it is still relevant
- the claim does not pretend the study is new
- current sources are used alongside it when needed

Example: an older resistance-training study may support an evidence-history point, but current research should still carry present-day practice claims where available.

### Tier 4: The Book

Use the book only for:

- credibility framing
- category history
- book-page content
- the platform-as-second-edition narrative
- explaining why StrongPath exists
- directing readers to the book as an authority asset

Do not use the book to substantiate specific current health claims.

### Tier 5: Unsourced Assertions

Unsourced assertions are not acceptable for substantive claims.

If a claim matters and cannot be cited:

1. find a credible third-party source
2. rewrite the claim to a supported narrower version
3. cut the claim

## 4. Citation Classification System

Every citation should be classified by the job it performs.

| Class | Use For | Typical Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical | Safety, intervention, disease-adjacent, post-fall, post-surgery, contraindication, or clinician-visible claims | Peer-reviewed studies, clinical guidelines, NIH/NIA, ACSM, Mayo Clinic |
| Institutional | Patient-facing definitions, public-health context, general safety guidance | Harvard Health Publishing, Mayo Clinic, NIH, NIA, academic medical centers |
| Consensus | Claims about what the field broadly supports or recommends | Reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, multiple aligned institutional sources |
| Mechanism | Explaining why resistance training, protein, recovery, or progression matters | Peer-reviewed physiology research, reviews, academic sources |
| Historical | Category history, landmark findings, book context, StrongPath origin | Landmark studies, the book as credibility anchor, governance-approved founder narrative |
| Commerce-sensitive | Claims adjacent to products, programs, affiliate links, supplements, quiz outputs, or recommendations | Current third-party research plus clear product-claim separation |

One source may carry more than one class, but the draft should know which job the citation is doing.

## 5. Claims That Require Citations

Cite any claim that is:

- quantitative
- medical, clinical, physiological, or safety-related
- about what research shows
- about risk, prevalence, disease, diagnosis, prevention, treatment, reversal, recovery, or outcomes
- about resistance training efficacy
- about protein, creatine, vitamin D, leucine, or any supplement/nutrition intervention
- about older adults, deconditioned adults, post-fall adults, post-surgery adults, or adults with chronic conditions
- surprising, counterintuitive, or likely to be challenged
- used to support a product, program, quiz, or affiliate recommendation

Examples that require citations:

- adults lose muscle mass with age
- resistance training can improve strength in adults over 80
- protein requirements may change with age
- walking alone is insufficient to preserve strength
- strength training is associated with reduced fall risk
- sarcopenia has a clinical definition or diagnostic status

## 6. Claims That May Not Require Citations

Some claims may stand without a formal citation when they are editorial, navigational, or plainly experiential.

Examples:

- StrongPath is built for adults who refuse to accept muscle loss as inevitable
- this article explains how to start
- this page is for caregivers evaluating a path for a parent
- we recommend starting with the article on sarcopenia

Even when no citation is required, the language must still follow `BRAND.md`: serious, direct, hopeful, and underpromising.

## 7. Claim Language Rules

StrongPath does not overclaim.

### Preferred Verbs

Use verbs such as:

- may help
- is associated with
- research suggests
- evidence supports
- can improve
- can support
- is linked to
- helps explain
- contributes to
- is one factor in

### Restricted Or Forbidden Verbs

Do not use, except when quoting or reproducing the book title exactly:

- cure
- treat
- reverse
- prevent, when attached to disease or product claims
- guarantee
- transform
- eliminate
- restore, when used as a promise
- fix

The word "reversing" appears only inside the italicized full title *Choosing the StrongPath: Reversing the Downward Spiral of Aging*. It is not used in StrongPath's own voice as a promise about the platform, programs, products, or reader outcomes.

## 8. Book Citation Standard

The book is a credibility anchor, not the citation layer.

### First Mention

First mention on a page uses the full title:

*Choosing the StrongPath: Reversing the Downward Spiral of Aging*

When relevant, pair the title with:

"an Amazon bestseller in Aging, Weight Training, Exercise, and Longevity"

### Subsequent Mentions

Subsequent mentions on the same page use:

*Choosing the StrongPath*

### Author Presence

Author names appear only where `BRAND.md` permits:

- the homepage book section credit line
- the Brief 10 book page credit line
- inside the book cover image wherever the cover is displayed

Do not place author names in:

- article opener parentheticals
- third-party citations
- brand-voice prose
- email signatures
- paid creative
- social bios
- article reference lists unless the page is specifically the book page or a direct bibliographic context approved by governance

### What The Book Can Support

The book can support:

- that the topic has a serious history inside StrongPath
- that StrongPath extends the work the book started
- that the book is the category credibility asset
- that the book is available for readers who want the deeper origin text

The book cannot support:

- a 2026 claim about current science
- a product promise
- a program outcome
- a supplement claim
- a safety claim
- a medical or disease-related assertion

If a draft says "the book shows that X" and X is a specific health claim, rewrite it to cite the underlying current third-party source directly.

## 9. Book/Product Firewall

The firewall protects credibility, reader trust, and legal posture.

### Rule 1: Research Claims Do Not Attach To Products

An article may explain what research shows about resistance training, protein, recovery, or sarcopenia.

That does not allow a StrongPath product, affiliate product, quiz, program, or future supplement to inherit the research claim as its own promise.

### Rule 2: Products May Claim Only What They Actually Do

Product-facing language may describe:

- format
- included materials
- intended audience
- educational purpose
- user experience
- what the program helps organize or guide

Product-facing language may not claim:

- disease treatment
- disease prevention
- reversal of aging
- guaranteed strength gain
- fall prevention as a product outcome
- medical recovery
- clinical equivalence to physical therapy or medical care

### Rule 3: The Book Does Not Launder Product Claims

Do not use the book's authority to make a product claim feel substantiated.

Bad pattern:

- The book is credible.
- The book discusses sarcopenia.
- Therefore this StrongPath product can claim sarcopenia outcomes.

Correct pattern:

- The book establishes the category and the mission.
- Current third-party research substantiates educational claims.
- Product claims remain narrower than research claims.

### Rule 4: Affiliate Products Require Extra Discipline

Affiliate content must:

- disclose affiliate relationships where required
- use the correct Amazon Associates tag when linking to Amazon
- separate educational evidence from purchase recommendation
- avoid implying that a product produces clinical outcomes
- prefer "useful tool for executing the protocol" over "solution to the condition"

## 10. Article-Level Reference Handling

Every substantive article should make its evidence visible enough that a skeptical reader can see the discipline without feeling dropped into a medical journal.

### Minimum Article Standard

Each substantive article must include:

- citations for every major factual or health claim
- named sources in the body where the claim is important
- a reference section or source list when the article relies on multiple studies
- links to institutional or study sources where available
- citation freshness appropriate to the claim
- a clear separation between book credibility and research substantiation

### Inline Citation Style

Use readable, patient-facing attribution in the body.

Preferred patterns:

- "A 2023 review in [Journal] found..."
- "Harvard Health Publishing explains..."
- "NIH describes sarcopenia as..."
- "In a landmark study, Fiatarone and colleagues showed..."
- "Current protein research suggests..."

Avoid citation clutter that makes the article feel like an academic paper. The reader should trust the evidence without having to decode it.

### Reference Section Style

When an article uses a reference list, include enough information to identify and verify the source:

- author or institution
- year or last reviewed date when available
- title
- journal or publishing institution
- link

For institutional pages, prefer:

- institution name
- article title
- last reviewed or updated date when available
- access date when the page is undated or likely to change

### Source Placement

Place the citation close to the claim it supports.

Do not gather citations at the end of the article in a way that makes it unclear which source supports which claim.

### Citation Density

Use enough citations to substantiate the article, not enough to perform seriousness.

Rules of thumb:

- one source cannot carry an entire article unless the article is explicitly about that source
- a pillar article usually needs multiple sources
- a narrow FAQ may need one or two sources
- a product page should cite research only when making educational claims, and should keep product claims separate

### Minimum Expectations By Content Type

| Content Type | Minimum Citation Expectation |
|---|---|
| Pillar article | Multiple sources across clinical, institutional, consensus, and mechanism classes; reference list required. |
| Satellite article | Enough sources to support the main claim and any safety or commerce-sensitive language; reference list preferred. |
| FAQ | One strong source per substantive answer, unless the answer is purely navigational. |
| Product page | Citations only for educational claims; product claims must stay uncited if they are simply describing the product. |
| Quiz result page | Cite educational or risk-context claims; do not imply diagnosis, treatment, or individualized medical advice. |
| Email | Cite or link when making a specific health claim; otherwise route to a cited article. |
| Social post | Avoid dense citations; link to the cited article that substantiates the claim. |

## 11. Outbound-Link Discipline

Outbound links are required when a source carries a substantive claim and a public source is available. Link directly to the study, institutional page, guideline, or book/product destination being referenced.

Do not link out to:

- AI-generated answers
- supplement-brand evidence pages
- retailer pages as evidence
- competitor marketing pages
- forums, comments, or social posts as substantiation
- paywalled abstracts when a better public source is available
- affiliate destinations in the same sentence as a health claim

Affiliate links must be separated from evidence links. Evidence explains the claim; affiliate links support a buying action. Do not make an affiliate product the visible proof for an educational or health claim.

## 12. Evidence Freshness

StrongPath's platform-as-second-edition framing depends on being demonstrably current.

### Use Current Sources When

- making present-day health claims
- describing current consensus
- discussing protein, supplements, resistance training, fall risk, or safety
- writing clinician-visible content
- updating a pillar page

### Older Sources Are Acceptable When

- the source is a landmark study
- the claim is historical
- the finding remains central to the field
- the article names the date honestly
- newer sources are used where current practice or consensus is at issue

### Review Trigger

A citation should be reviewed when:

- it is more than five years old and supports a current practice claim
- it is an institutional page without a visible updated date
- it supports a claim in a high-traffic pillar article
- the article touches safety, disease, supplements, or post-event recovery
- newer evidence may materially change the recommendation

## 13. Prohibited Source Use

Do not use as substantiation:

- AI-generated answers
- unsourced blogs
- influencer content
- supplement-brand claims
- retailer product pages
- Amazon reviews
- testimonials
- Reddit or forum comments
- competitor marketing pages
- the book as proof for current health claims
- a study abstract when the full claim depends on details not visible in the abstract

These may be used for audience language, objections, or market awareness when appropriate, but not as evidence for StrongPath claims.

## 14. Keyword And Search Claim Discipline

`keyword-universe.md` identifies search demand. Search demand does not authorize claim language.

Rules:

- a keyword may appear in research or metadata without becoming the article's public claim
- "senior" and "elderly" may appear only where persona rules allow them
- "prevent" keywords must be rewritten into education-safe language when needed
- "reverse" keywords must not be echoed in StrongPath's own voice
- high-volume unsafe keywords are excluded even when commercially tempting
- fall-prevention, frailty, surgery, hospitalization, and supplement queries require higher substantiation and stronger hedging

SEO follows evidence discipline. Evidence discipline does not bend to SEO.

## 15. Persona-Specific Evidence Expectations

### Margaret: Pre-Frail Adult

Margaret trusts:

- visible citation discipline
- clear mechanisms
- underpromising language
- an editorial register
- the book as credibility signal

Do not talk down to her. Do not call her elderly. Do not give generic wellness advice where a specific mechanism or protocol is needed.

### David: Proactive Caregiver

David trusts:

- a credible path
- goodwill-led language
- safety clarity
- named research
- honest acknowledgement of limits

Caregiver-facing evidence must reduce overwhelm. It should not use fear to force action.

### Dr. Chen: Allied Health Professional

Dr. Chen trusts:

- named peer-reviewed studies
- author, year, and journal where applicable
- current institutional sources
- honest limits
- clear statement that StrongPath is not a clinical service, diagnostic tool, medical device, or replacement for physical therapy

Clinician-visible pages require the highest citation discipline. Half-credible clinical material is worse than no clinical material.

## 16. Safety, Medical, And Disclaimer Rules

Any article involving safety, contraindications, deconditioned adults, chronic conditions, post-fall recovery, post-surgery rebuilding, hospitalization, frailty, supplements, or adults in their 80s and 90s must:

- cite credible third-party sources
- hedge appropriately
- distinguish education from medical advice
- advise readers to work with a physician, physical therapist, or qualified clinician when relevant
- avoid product-attached clinical claims
- avoid implying StrongPath replaces clinical care

Medical disclaimers are required on health pages under brand legal rules. This document defines citation standards; it does not define CMS placement or template implementation.

## 17. Review Rules For Drafts

Before a draft is review-ready, check:

- every specific health claim has a source
- every quantitative claim has a source
- the book is not used as a source for specific health claims
- product claims are narrower than research claims
- affiliate recommendations do not imply clinical outcomes
- citations are close to the claims they support
- citation classes are appropriate to the claim
- outbound evidence links are separated from affiliate links
- dated sources are still defensible
- older landmark studies are named as older
- "reverse" appears only inside the italicized book title
- "senior" and "elderly" follow persona rules
- no author parentheticals are used for the book outside permitted surfaces
- no current medical advisor, clinician, scientist, or active author role is implied where none exists
- all claims are serious, direct, hopeful, and underpromised

Failure on any item means revise before founder review.

## 18. Escalation Rules

Escalate before publishing when:

- a claim may be legal, medical, FTC, FDA, or affiliate-risk sensitive
- available sources conflict
- a claim depends on a weak or outdated source
- a draft needs to cite the book in a way not covered here
- author names would appear outside permitted surfaces
- a product page wants to use research as conversion proof
- a clinician-facing article lacks clinical-grade support
- a keyword opportunity pushes toward unsafe claim language
- a source cannot be verified
- evidence and affiliate destinations are difficult to separate cleanly

Do not silently improvise around evidence conflicts.

## 19. Operational Defaults

When uncertain:

- underpromise
- cite the current third-party source
- separate credibility from substantiation
- keep product claims narrow
- use mechanism language
- name the limit
- cut the claim if support is weak

StrongPath's authority compounds only if the citation record stays clean.
