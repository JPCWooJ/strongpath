# CONTENT_BRIEF_STANDARD.md

## Purpose

Canonical Layer 2 article/content brief standard for StrongPath.

This file defines the minimum inputs required before drafting StrongPath editorial content: article type, persona, search intent, keyword and cluster role, approved claims, prohibited claims, evidence, source packet, internal links, CTA direction, commerce boundaries, tone/brand guidance, risk flags, founder questions, approval, revision, and completeness.

This is an editorial governance document. It does not define CMS implementation systems, project-management systems, analytics/reporting systems, AI automation architecture, or enterprise documentation overhead.

## 1. Core Principle

A StrongPath brief is a drafting contract.

It should give the writer enough direction to produce a useful, evidence-safe, persona-aware article without guessing. It should also give reviewers a clear baseline for QA, scoring, and founder review.

Briefs should be short, specific, and operational. Do not turn briefs into essays.

## 2. Brief Purpose

The brief exists to define:

- who the article is for
- what reader problem it answers
- what search or editorial intent it serves
- what role it plays in the topic cluster
- which claims are allowed
- which claims are prohibited
- what evidence is required
- where the article should link
- what CTA pathway is appropriate
- what risks need review
- what founder decisions remain open

If the brief cannot answer these questions, the article is not ready for drafting.

## 3. Article-Type Definitions

Use one primary article type per brief.

### Pillar Article

Broad authority article that anchors a topic cluster. Requires deeper research, stronger source packet, clear internal-link plan, and likely founder review.

### Satellite Article

Focused supporting article answering one narrower query or reader problem. Must link back to the relevant pillar and avoid becoming a duplicate pillar.

### FAQ

Short answer content for specific questions. Must be citation-safe when making health, mechanism, safety, or product-adjacent claims.

### Book/Authority Page

Content centered on the book, founder, or StrongPath authority model. Must not use the book as evidence for current clinical claims.

### Product/Support Page

Commerce-sensitive page that explains a product, offer, quiz, book format, or support pathway. Must preserve evidence and commerce separation.

### Refresh/Update Brief

Brief for revising an existing article. Must identify what changed, what remains valid, what needs re-verification, and what state the article should return to.

## 4. Required Brief Fields

Every brief must include:

- working title
- article type
- primary persona
- persona moment
- reader question
- search intent
- target keyword or phrase
- cluster role
- target pillar or satellite relationship
- approved claims
- do-not-claim list
- evidence requirements
- source-packet location or status
- internal-link plan
- CTA direction
- commerce-boundary notes
- tone/brand notes
- risk and escalation flags
- founder questions, if any
- approval state
- owner or next responsible function

Optional fields are allowed only when they make drafting clearer.

## 5. Persona Definition Requirements

Persona fields must name:

- primary persona from `PERSONAS.md`
- reader's current concern or motivation
- reader's likely fear, objection, or confusion
- desired level of specificity
- language to avoid
- practical next step the reader can take

If a topic serves multiple personas, pick one primary persona and list secondary personas only as constraints.

Do not write generic wellness content for an unnamed reader.

## 6. Search-Intent Definition

Search intent follows `SEO_SYSTEMS.md`.

The brief must state:

- intent type: informational, comparison, navigational, commercial-support, or mixed
- reader's expected answer
- what the article should resolve quickly
- what the article should not try to resolve
- whether the query carries health, legal, emotional, or commerce sensitivity

Search intent informs structure. It does not justify unsafe claims.

## 7. Keyword And Cluster Fields

Keyword fields must include:

- primary keyword or phrase
- secondary terms, if any
- cluster name
- pillar/satellite role
- related articles to support or avoid duplicating
- internal-link targets
- terms to avoid because they create unsafe promise, diagnosis, or treatment framing

Keyword usage should sound natural and reader-first.

## 8. Approved-Claim Fields

Approved claims are the claims the writer may make without further research.

Each approved claim should include:

- claim wording or claim boundary
- evidence class
- source reference or research-packet pointer
- required hedge or limitation
- whether citation is required in the article

Approved claims should be narrow enough to draft safely. If the claim needs more confidence than the source supports, revise the claim before drafting.

## 9. Do-Not-Claim Fields

Every brief must list prohibited claim territory.

Common do-not-claim categories:

- diagnosis, treatment, prevention, cure, or reversal
- guaranteed outcomes
- supplement or product efficacy beyond evidence
- book-as-clinical-proof language
- fear-based urgency
- clinician-level recommendations without appropriate authority
- unsupported mechanism claims
- overbroad aging, frailty, fall, or strength claims

If the writer needs a prohibited claim to make the article work, the topic must return to research or escalation.

## 10. Evidence And Source-Packet Requirements

Evidence requirements follow `RESEARCH_SYSTEMS.md` and `CITATION_STANDARD.md`.

The brief must state:

- whether a research packet is required
- source classes needed
- citation classifications expected
- source freshness expectations
- claims requiring visible citations
- claims requiring institutional or consensus support
- claims that may use mechanism support only as background
- commerce-sensitive evidence boundaries

Do not attach raw links without extraction notes. A source packet should tell the writer what the source supports and what it does not support.

## 11. Internal-Link Planning

Internal links follow `INTERNAL_LINKING_SYSTEM.md`.

The brief must identify:

- required pillar link
- required satellite links
- optional related-content links
- book or authority page link, if appropriate
- evidence links distinct from internal links
- commerce links distinct from education links
- anchor text guidance
- orphan-page prevention need

Internal links should create a useful reader pathway, not a link dump.

## 12. CTA Planning

CTA direction must state:

- primary CTA, if any
- secondary CTA, if any
- whether CTA is educational, book, product, quiz, newsletter, or support pathway
- where CTA should appear
- what claim boundary applies
- what CTA should not imply

CTA placement must follow article intent. Education comes before commerce.

## 13. Commerce-Boundary Definitions

Commerce-boundary fields are required for product pages, book pages, quiz pages, affiliate-sensitive topics, supplement-adjacent topics, and any article with commercial CTAs.

The brief must define:

- commerce-sensitive sections
- affiliate or product mentions allowed
- affiliate or product mentions prohibited
- evidence that may not be used as product proof
- wording to avoid
- disclosure or separation needs

Commerce may guide next steps. It may not substantiate health claims.

## 14. Tone/Brand Guidance Fields

Tone and brand guidance follow `BRAND.md`.

Briefs should specify:

- desired tone: serious, clear, hopeful, practical
- reader confidence level
- language to use
- language to avoid
- sensitivity notes
- whether founder/book authority should be foregrounded, backgrounded, or omitted

Avoid vague instructions such as "make it inspiring." Name the actual behavior the prose should have.

## 15. Risk And Escalation Fields

Risk fields must identify:

- medical or legal sensitivity
- evidence uncertainty
- commerce sensitivity
- persona sensitivity
- brand or claim risk
- SEO pressure toward unsafe phrasing
- founder judgment need

Escalate before drafting when the brief depends on unresolved risk.

Escalation output should state the issue, risk, options, and recommended decision.

## 16. Founder-Question Fields

Founder questions should be few and decision-ready.

Use founder questions for:

- strategic positioning
- book or author framing
- pillar article direction
- product, quiz, or program claims
- sensitive persona framing
- unresolved risk tradeoffs
- governance conflicts

Do not send founder questions that can be resolved by existing governance.

## 17. Brief Approval Workflow

Brief states:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Draft Brief | Direction exists but is not ready for drafting. |
| Research Needed | Evidence or claim boundaries are incomplete. |
| Escalation Needed | A risk or decision blocks approval. |
| Revision Needed | Brief has enough shape but needs correction. |
| Approved Brief | Drafting may begin. |

Approval requires:

- article type is clear
- persona is clear
- search intent is clear
- claims and do-not-claims are defined
- evidence requirements are satisfied or assigned
- internal-link and CTA direction are safe
- commerce boundaries are explicit when relevant
- founder questions are resolved or clearly routed

## 18. Revision And Update Workflow

Revise a brief when:

- research changes
- source freshness changes
- article scope changes
- keyword or intent changes
- persona changes
- internal-link plan changes
- CTA or commerce role changes
- QA or scoring reveals a brief-level problem
- founder direction changes

Update briefs should preserve the original article purpose unless the article is being repositioned. If repositioning is required, create a new approved brief state before rewriting.

## 19. Brief Completeness Standards

A brief is complete when a writer can answer:

- what am I writing?
- who am I writing for?
- what should this article accomplish?
- what can I claim?
- what must I not claim?
- what evidence must I use?
- what links and CTAs belong here?
- what risks need care?
- what decisions are already made?

If a competent writer would need to invent core direction, the brief is incomplete.

## 20. Operational Defaults

When uncertain:

- narrow the scope
- name the persona
- define the claim boundary
- require research
- separate commerce from evidence
- reduce CTA pressure
- choose safer keywords
- escalate founder-level decisions

The best brief makes the draft easier to write and easier to review.
