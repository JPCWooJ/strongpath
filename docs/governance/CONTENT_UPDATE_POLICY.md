# Content Update Policy

## Purpose

- Keep StrongPath content accurate, current, linked, and authority-preserving.
- Define when to refresh, merge, replace, deprecate, or archive.
- Avoid maintenance theater.

## Core Rules

- Update to preserve trust, not to look active.
- Freshness depends on risk, not age alone.
- Preserve strong URLs unless there is a durable reason to change them.
- Merge overlap instead of multiplying weak pages.
- Run QA proportional to the change.
- Do not leave stale or risky content in active pathways.

## Review Triggers

- Evidence changes.
- Source link breaks, redirects, or changes materially.
- Source age exceeds citation standards.
- Governance changes affect claims, links, commerce, or positioning.
- Article overlaps a newer or stronger page.
- Keyword intent or article role changes.
- Metadata, headings, CTA, or anchors overclaim.
- Commerce drifts toward evidence claims.
- Founder, book, product, quiz, or platform framing changes.
- Medical, legal, brand, persona, or citation risk appears.

## Freshness Cadence

- Pillars: light review every six months; full claim/citation review annually.
- Strategic, safety, supplement, commerce-sensitive, or high-traffic satellites: review every six months.
- Ordinary satellites: review annually.
- Immediate review when parent pillar, source, governance, or risk changes.

## Citation Revalidation

- List claims with citations.
- Open each source.
- Confirm source resolves.
- Confirm title, institution/author, and date.
- Confirm source supports exact claim.
- Confirm source class matches claim risk.
- Confirm freshness.
- Revise, replace, hedge, or cut unsupported claims.

## Broken Links

- Identify link type: citation, internal, commerce, book, CTA.
- Replace citation only with a source supporting the same claim.
- Route internal link to best current page.
- Keep commerce separate from evidence.
- Remove links that no longer serve the reader.
- Rerun affected QA.

## Merge / Replace / Deprecate

Merge when:

- two pages answer same reader, intent, and claim territory
- a newer or stronger page supersedes an older page
- authority is split unnecessarily

Replace when:

- reader need remains but page is too weak, stale, or misaligned to patch

Deprecate or archive when:

- article is inaccurate
- evidence cannot support it
- topic is out of scope
- page creates medical, legal, brand, commerce, or authority risk
- maintenance cost exceeds strategic value

## Redirect Rules

- Use redirect when a clear replacement exists or a URL changed for a durable reason.
- Do not delete or redirect major pages without checking internal links and authority pathway.
- Update internal links after merge, replacement, or deprecation.

## Update QA

Run affected checks when changes touch:

- claims
- citations
- links
- headings
- metadata
- CTA
- commerce or affiliate language
- persona framing
- book, founder, author, product, quiz, or program framing

Run full QA and rescore for substantial rewrites, repositioning, cluster-role changes, or commerce-path changes.

## Maintenance States

- Current: no known issue.
- Monitor: known uncertainty.
- Refresh Needed: claim, citation, link, or positioning update required.
- Revision Needed: writing or structure issue found.
- Merge Candidate: overlap likely.
- Replacement Needed: role valid, page should be rebuilt.
- Deprecation Needed: page should leave active guidance.
- Escalation Needed: risk blocks decision.

## Pass / Fail

Pass when:

- content remains accurate, sourced, linked, and claim-safe
- update scope is narrow
- affected QA passed
- internal pathways remain clean

Fail when:

- stale source carries current guidance
- broken citation has no replacement and claim remains
- duplicate pages remain active
- deprecated page stays in active pathways
- update changes risk without escalation

## Escalate

- Evidence is stale, conflicting, or insufficient.
- Source replacement changes claim meaning.
- Medical/legal risk appears.
- Merge, redirect, replacement, or deprecation affects a major page.
- Commerce pressure affects editorial judgment.
- Founder, book, author, product, quiz, or program framing changes.
- Reviewers disagree.
