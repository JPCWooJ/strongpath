# CONTENT_UPDATE_POLICY.md

## Purpose

Canonical Layer 2 content freshness, maintenance, refresh, update, merge, deprecation, and archival policy for StrongPath.

This file defines how StrongPath keeps published content current, citation-safe, internally coherent, and authority-preserving over time.

This is an editorial governance document. It does not define CMS implementation architecture, analytics/reporting systems, AI automation systems, or enterprise maintenance bureaucracy.

## 1. Core Principle

StrongPath content should become more trustworthy over time.

Updates exist to preserve accuracy, usefulness, citation integrity, internal-link clarity, and authority. Do not refresh content only to look active. Do not leave stale content in active reader pathways when it can mislead the reader or weaken StrongPath's authority.

## 2. Freshness Standards

Freshness is based on risk, not age alone.

High-freshness content includes:

- pillar articles
- health, safety, mechanism, fall-risk, frailty, supplement, surgery, hospitalization, or contraindication content
- commerce-sensitive articles
- product, quiz, affiliate, or book pathway pages
- articles cited by many internal pages
- pages that define StrongPath authority

Lower-risk content may remain stable longer when claims, links, and positioning remain accurate.

Current-practice claims need current sources. Historical or mechanism background may use older sources when labeled and contextually appropriate.

## 3. Update Triggers

Trigger review when:

- cited evidence changes materially
- source links break, redirect unexpectedly, or change content
- source age exceeds the rules in this policy
- governance files change in a way that affects the article
- article overlaps a newer or stronger page
- keyword intent or article role changes
- internal links create orphan risk or outdated pathways
- metadata, headings, or CTAs overclaim against current standards
- commerce language drifts toward evidence claims
- founder, book, product, quiz, or platform framing changes
- reviewer identifies medical, legal, brand, or persona risk

Any team member or agent may flag a trigger. The next action should be narrow and explicit.

## 4. Source-Age Review Rules

Source age review follows `CITATION_STANDARD.md`.

Default rules:

- clinical, institutional, consensus, and commerce-sensitive claims require the freshest practical support
- safety and current-practice claims should be reviewed when sources are more than five years old
- older landmark studies may remain when clearly framed as historical or foundational
- mechanism sources may remain when they support stable background and do not carry current-practice recommendations
- quantitative claims should be rechecked during any material update

If an article's strongest source is stale, revise the claim, find better support, or escalate.

## 5. Pillar Refresh Cadence

Pillar articles require scheduled editorial attention.

Minimum cadence:

- light review every six months
- full citation and claim review at least annually
- immediate review when major source, governance, or positioning changes occur

Pillar review must check:

- article purpose
- cluster role
- source freshness
- citation accuracy
- internal-link coverage
- metadata and headings
- CTA safety
- overlap with newer pages
- founder-review need

Pillars should not become stale hubs.

## 6. Satellite Refresh Cadence

Satellite articles require lighter but real maintenance.

Minimum cadence:

- review at least annually for ordinary satellites
- review every six months for safety, supplement, commerce-sensitive, or high-traffic strategic satellites
- review immediately when their parent pillar changes materially

Satellite review must check:

- answer still matches search intent
- parent pillar link is current
- claims remain narrow
- citations still support the article
- links are not broken or misleading
- article is not redundant with another satellite

If a satellite has outgrown its scope, narrow it, merge it, or consider whether the cluster needs a new pillar.

## 7. Citation Revalidation Workflow

Citation revalidation checks whether each source still supports the claim it is attached to.

Workflow:

1. list claims with visible citations
2. open each source
3. confirm the source resolves
4. confirm title, institution or author, and year
5. confirm the source still supports the exact claim
6. confirm source class matches claim class
7. check freshness requirement
8. revise, replace, hedge, or cut unsupported claims

Do not swap citations mechanically. If the claim changes, the article must change.

## 8. Broken-Link Workflow

Broken links are trust issues.

Workflow:

1. identify link type: citation, internal, commerce, book, or CTA
2. confirm whether the link is broken, redirected, gated, or materially changed
3. replace citation links only with sources that support the same claim
4. route internal links to the best current page
5. separate commerce links from evidence links
6. remove links that no longer serve the reader
7. rerun affected QA checks

If a broken citation cannot be replaced, revise or cut the claim.

## 9. Overlap And Merge Workflow

Overlap review is required when two pages answer the same reader, same intent, and same claim territory.

Merge when:

- one page is clearly stronger
- two thin pages should become one useful page
- a newer page supersedes an older page
- internal links split authority unnecessarily
- the reader would not understand why both pages exist

Do not merge when two pages serve distinct personas, intents, or cluster roles.

Merge output must identify:

- surviving page
- content to preserve
- claims to revalidate
- internal links to update
- redirect or deprecation recommendation
- founder-review need, if any

## 10. Article Replacement Workflow

Replacement is appropriate when an article's role is still needed but the existing page is too weak, stale, or misaligned to patch.

Replacement workflow:

1. confirm the reader need still matters
2. create or update the brief
3. rebuild research packet if needed
4. draft replacement within current governance
5. QA and score the replacement
6. update internal links
7. deprecate or redirect the old page as appropriate

Do not keep both pages active unless they serve different intents.

## 11. Redirect And Deprecation Rules

Redirects and deprecations preserve reader trust and accumulated authority.

Use redirect when:

- old page has a clear replacement
- article was merged into a stronger page
- URL changed with a durable reason
- keeping the old page active would create duplicate or stale guidance

Use deprecation without active replacement when:

- topic is outside current StrongPath scope
- evidence cannot support the article safely
- claim territory is no longer appropriate
- page undermines brand or authority

Deprecation note must state the reason and internal-link cleanup required.

## 12. Archive Criteria

Archive content when it should no longer guide active readers.

Archive when:

- article is inaccurate or no longer supportable
- article is superseded by a stronger page
- article belongs to a prior strategy
- article creates medical, legal, brand, or commerce risk
- maintenance cost exceeds strategic value
- article cannot be safely refreshed

Archived content is historical reference, not active guidance.

## 13. Authority-Preservation Rules

Maintenance should protect the authority StrongPath has earned.

Rules:

- preserve strong URLs unless there is a clear reason to change them
- merge overlap instead of multiplying weak pages
- keep pillars current before expanding clusters
- update internal links after every merge, replacement, or deprecation
- preserve useful citations and replace weak ones
- avoid deleting pages without checking replacement pathways
- keep book and product pathways claim-safe

Authority is preserved by making the site easier to trust, not by preserving every page forever.

## 14. Update QA Requirements

Every material update requires QA proportional to the change.

Run affected checks from `CONTENT_QA.md` when changes touch:

- claims
- citations
- source links
- headings
- metadata
- internal links
- CTA language
- commerce or affiliate language
- persona framing
- book, founder, author, product, quiz, or program framing

Run full QA when the article is substantially rewritten or repositioned.

## 15. Rescore And Re-Review Triggers

Rescore under `ARTICLE_SCORING_RUBRIC.md` when:

- article is materially rewritten
- claims or citations change substantially
- article type or cluster role changes
- commerce pathway changes
- internal-link architecture changes
- founder gate is newly required
- prior score is no longer meaningful

Minor typo, formatting, or broken non-evidence link fixes do not require full rescoring unless they reveal a broader issue.

## 16. Founder-Review Refresh Triggers

Founder review is required during refresh when:

- pillar positioning changes
- book, founder, author, or platform framing changes
- commerce, product, quiz, or program pathway changes
- David-facing or emotionally sensitive framing changes
- medical/legal risk appears
- article replacement or deprecation affects a key authority page
- reviewers disagree on merge, archive, or claim-risk decision

Founder review should receive the decision needed, not a general request to inspect the page.

## 17. Workflow States

Use one maintenance state at a time.

| State | Meaning | Next Action |
|---|---|---|
| Current | No known maintenance issue. | Keep active. |
| Monitor | Watch for known source, claim, or strategy uncertainty. | Review at next cadence or trigger. |
| Refresh Needed | Article requires citation, claim, link, or positioning update. | Assign targeted refresh. |
| Revision Needed | Update revealed article-level writing or structure issues. | Revise and rerun affected QA. |
| Merge Candidate | Article may overlap another page. | Run overlap/merge workflow. |
| Replacement Needed | Role remains valid, but article should be rebuilt. | Create/update brief and replacement. |
| Deprecation Needed | Page should leave active guidance. | Decide redirect, archive, or removal path. |
| Escalation Needed | Risk or governance conflict blocks maintenance decision. | Escalate before action. |

Do not mark stale or risky content `Current`.

## 18. Escalation Rules

Escalate when:

- evidence is stale, conflicting, or insufficient
- medical/legal risk appears during refresh
- source replacement would change claim meaning
- article overlap affects pillar strategy
- redirect or deprecation affects a major page
- commerce pressure affects editorial judgment
- keyword opportunity pushes unsafe update language
- founder, book, author, product, quiz, or program framing changes
- reviewers disagree on current, merge, replace, or archive status

Escalation output must state:

- page
- issue
- risk type
- options: refresh, hedge, source, merge, replace, redirect, deprecate, archive, defer
- recommended decision

## 19. Operational Defaults

When uncertain:

- preserve trust over traffic
- update the claim before polishing prose
- replace weak sources
- merge overlap
- keep internal pathways clean
- avoid unnecessary URL changes
- run targeted QA
- rescore material changes
- escalate before deleting authority

StrongPath maintenance should feel calm, current, and deliberate.
