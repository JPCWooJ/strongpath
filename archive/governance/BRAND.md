## 1. Positioning Statement

**StrongPath is the evidence-based strength platform for adults who refuse to accept muscle loss as inevitable — built on the same research foundation behind *Choosing the StrongPath: Reversing the Downward Spiral of Aging*, an Amazon bestseller in Aging, Weight Training, Exercise, and Longevity.**

This sentence is the north star. If a campaign, article, or product does not ladder back to it, it does not belong in the brand.

### Unpacking the sentence

- **"Evidence-based"** — Every claim we make cites current peer-reviewed research, a named institutional source (Harvard Health, Mayo Clinic, NIH, academic institutions), or a mechanism. We are the serious option. Competitors shout; we cite.
- **"Strength platform"** — Not a blog, not a supplement brand, not an app. A platform that will grow to include content, programs, a membership, an AI advisor, and eventually supplements. The platform is the second edition of the work *Choosing the StrongPath* started — same intellectual project, current medium. The book did the work the book could do in 2018; the platform does the work a website, app, and AI-enabled advisor can do in 2026 and beyond.
- **"Adults who refuse to accept"** — The self-selection signal. Our customer has decided decline is not the plan. We resource the resolved; we do not try to convert the resigned.
- **"Muscle loss as inevitable"** — Names the enemy (sarcopenia) without using a clinical word that loses most readers. The fight is against inevitability, not aging.
- **"The same research foundation behind *Choosing the StrongPath: Reversing the Downward Spiral of Aging*"** — The credibility anchor. The book is the category-defining authority asset that proves the topic is serious, the framework is real, and the research is deep. Our own content cites current third-party research directly; the book stands for the category we operate in, not as a citation source for specific claims.
- **"Amazon bestseller in Aging, Weight Training, Exercise, and Longevity"** — Four categories. Verified on the Amazon product page, defensible, credential-bearing. Not #1 — that is a goal, not a claim.

## 2. The Brand in Three Words

**Serious. Direct. Hopeful.**

In that order. When they conflict, earlier beats later.

- **Serious beats clever.** If a tagline is cute but not credible, it loses.
- **Direct beats comprehensive.** If a sentence can be cut, cut it.
- **Hopeful beats urgent.** We do not manufacture fear to sell. The reader leaves every page believing the path is walkable.

## 3. Voice

**The specialist, not the salesman.**

StrongPath sounds like the well-regarded specialist you get referred to — not the urgent care clinic, not the influencer, not the supplement brand. Calm, informed, a little dry. Confident enough to say "most of what you have read about aging is wrong" without hedging. Disciplined enough to never overclaim what the science supports.

### 3.1 Voice rules

1. **Cite mechanisms, not miracles.** Resistance training, protein synthesis, motor unit recruitment, recovery. These words build trust. "Transform your body in 30 days" destroys it.
2. **Write at a college reading level, not a medical journal.** We translate research. We are not performing it.
3. **Name the objection before the reader does.** "You have probably been told that strength training is dangerous after 60. The research says the opposite."
4. **Use "we," not "you should."** "We recommend…" is peer-level. "You should…" is parental.
5. **Short sentences carry weight. Long sentences carry nuance.** Alternate deliberately.
6. **The reader is intelligent. Write like it.** No talking down. No filler phrases like "let's face it" or "the truth is."

### 3.2 Words we use

*Strength. Capacity. Independence. Protocol. Research. Mechanism. Protein synthesis. Resistance. Recovery. Progression. Framework. Evidence. Path. Build. Maintain. Preserve. Sarcopenia (the muscle-mass side). Dynapenia (the strength side — the loss of force, as distinct from the loss of mass).*

### 3.3 Words we do not use

*Anti-aging. Miracle. Breakthrough. Cure. Reverse. Secret. Hack. Fountain of youth. Transformation. Journey (overused). Lifestyle. Wellness (too soft). Longevity (crowded buzzword — use carefully, not our primary frame).*

**Note on "reverse" / "reversing":** The word appears in the book's published subtitle (*Reversing the Downward Spiral of Aging*). It is allowed to appear inside an italicized book title when we cite the book — that is the authors' claim, not ours. It is **not** allowed in our own voice to describe what StrongPath, StrongPath programs, or StrongPath products do. The book/product firewall (Section 9.1) governs.

### 3.4 Phrases that fail the voice test

- "Don't let age slow you down!" — exclamation point, generic, performative
- "Unlock your best body at any age" — unlock, best body, both disqualifying
- "The anti-aging secret doctors don't want you to know" — fear, secret, conspiracy — three strikes
- "Transform your life in 30 days" — transform, timeline promise
- "As we get older…" — lazy opener, passive voice, no specificity

### 3.5 Phrases that pass

- "Sarcopenia is not a disease of aging. It is a disease of disuse, and the research is clear on what reverses it."
- "Most strength advice for people over 60 is either too cautious to work or too aggressive to be safe. There is a middle path, and it is well-documented."
- "You do not need to train like an athlete. You need to train like a person whose independence depends on it — because it does."

## 4. Tone by Audience

Voice is constant across StrongPath. Tone shifts by reader. Every asset identifies its primary audience before the first word is written.

### 4.1 The Pre-Frail Adult (55-72, end user)

**Tone:** Peer-level, respectful, specific.

The reader has noticed something — stairs are harder, groceries feel heavier, balance is not what it was. They are not broken. They are paying attention. We meet them as equals who have done the homework. We never call them "senior," "elderly," or "old." We address them by what they want to remain: strong, independent, capable.

*Example open:* "You have probably noticed your body behaving differently. You are not imagining it. Between ages 50 and 70, adults lose an average of 15% of their muscle mass — and more than half of their strength. This is measurable, it is predictable, and with the right protocol, it is largely preventable."

### 4.2 The Proactive Caregiver (45-60, adult child of an aging parent)

**Tone:** Reassuring and directive. Reduces overwhelm. Hands them a credible path.

The reader is watching a parent decline and feeling helpless. They have Googled, they are exhausted, they do not know who to trust. We become the trusted resource they can put in their parent's hands. Our job is to reduce their cognitive load.

*Example open:* "If you are watching a parent lose strength and independence, you already know the Google search for answers is overwhelming and mostly unhelpful. Here is what the research actually says about age-related muscle loss, and here is a program your parent can start this week."

### 4.3 The Allied Health Professional (PT, OT, geriatric RN, primary care)

**Tone:** Professional, research-forward, peer-to-peer.

The reader recommends resources to patients and cannot afford to recommend something that damages their reputation. We lead with credentialing and research. We never sell. We let the evidence sell.

*Example open:* "StrongPath is a patient-facing resource in the category defined by *Choosing the StrongPath: Reversing the Downward Spiral of Aging*, an Amazon bestseller in Aging, Weight Training, Exercise, and Longevity. This page is for clinicians evaluating whether to recommend us to patients."

## 5. Authority Hierarchy

**This is the moat.** Every public-facing page surfaces the hierarchy above the fold or within the first scroll. Per BP-06, this is non-negotiable — the single biggest gap in the prior build was that the authority was invisible.

Authority operates on two layers. **Credibility authority** establishes the brand's right to speak in the category. **Substantiation authority** proves the specific claims in any given piece of content. These are different jobs done by different inputs; they are not interchangeable. The two layers reinforce each other over time — sustained substantiation discipline builds the brand's credibility; the brand's credibility earns the reader's attention long enough for substantiation to do its work.

For Phase 1, no active medical advisor is on the brand. Credibility rests on the book, the platform-as-second-edition framing, and the brand's accumulating citation record. Substantiation rests on current third-party research. Overclaiming an active medical presence would be a credibility and legal risk.

### The two-layer authority model

**Layer 1 — Credibility authority.** Three elements compound into Layer 1, and each reinforces the others:

1. **The book — *Choosing the StrongPath: Reversing the Downward Spiral of Aging*.** An Amazon bestseller in Aging, Weight Training, Exercise, and Longevity. The bestseller fact is what makes the book a credibility asset for the brand. Market validation that the topic is serious, the framework was listened to, and the category is real. The book is not cited as a substantiation source for specific health claims inside our content.

2. **The platform as the second edition.** *Choosing the StrongPath* was published in 2018. The world has moved since — the research has expanded, sarcopenia received its ICD-10 code in 2016 and has moved further into mainstream awareness, and the tools available to translate research into practice now include websites, apps, and AI-enabled advisors that the book could not be. StrongPath is the second edition of the work the book started, in the form 2026 calls for. Not a literary second edition — an evolved-medium continuation of the same intellectual project.

3. **The citation record.** What a 2026 second edition is made of. Every piece of StrongPath content cites current peer-reviewed research, named institutional sources, and credible clinical guidance. This discipline is not per-article — it is brand-level. Over time, the cumulative citation record builds the brand's authority on the topic, and that authority flows back to the book it extends. The brand was early, in the form of the book; the field has caught up; current research now confirms and extends what the book argued. The brand's job is to demonstrate that continuity by citing rigorously.

**Layer 2 — Substantiation authority.** Every specific claim in our content — about sarcopenia prevalence, resistance training efficacy, protein requirements, recovery windows, intervention outcomes — is substantiated by a current, named, third-party source. Peer-reviewed studies, Harvard Health Publishing, Mayo Clinic, NIH, academic institutions. **This is what carries claims.** The book is not a citation for these; the book is part of Layer 1.

This two-layer model protects the brand against two different risks. Overclaiming on the credibility side would attach unsupported product claims to a credibility asset. Citing a 2018 book as the evidence base for 2026 health claims would be weak substantiation, vulnerable to FTC scrutiny, and intellectually dishonest when current research exists.

## 7. Messaging Pillars

Four pillars. Every piece of content — article, email, ad, video — ladders up to one of them. If it does not fit a pillar, it does not ship.

### Pillar 1 — Sarcopenia is real, measurable, and largely preventable

Our educational backbone. Defines the problem without fear. Anchors in data (15% muscle loss from 50-70, 30%+ of adults over 60 affected, ICD-10 code since 2016) and in the three mechanisms (resistance training, protein, recovery).

### Pillar 2 — Strength is the operating system of independence

Reframes strength from aesthetic to functional. The outcome the reader cares about is not abs — it is carrying groceries at 75, playing with grandchildren at 80, avoiding a nursing home at 85. Strength is the input; independence is the output. Carries the emotional weight.

### Pillar 3 — The research is clear; the execution is the hard part

Most readers have heard "lift weights and eat protein" before. Our job: package it into a protocol a 65-year-old can actually follow without hiring a trainer. Carries the programs and the AI advisor.

### Pillar 4 — You do not walk this alone

Community, trusted resources, a credible guide. The caregiver pillar. The antidote to the overwhelming Google search. Over time, carries the membership.

## 9. Legal and Compliance Guardrails

Sarcopenia is medical-adjacent. The FTC and FDA have jurisdiction over everything we publish that touches health. These rules are not suggestions.

### 9.1 The book/product firewall

**Three separate rules operate in parallel here, and all three matter.**

**Rule 1 — Book is credibility, not citation.** The book establishes that StrongPath operates in a serious, research-grounded category. It does not substantiate specific health claims in our content. Current third-party sources do that. A sentence of the form "*Choosing the StrongPath* shows that X" treats the book as a citation, which it is not in our model. Rewrite to cite the actual research: "A 2023 meta-analysis in *JAMA* shows that X" or "Harvard Health Publishing reports that X."

**Rule 2 — Claims attach to the book (education), not to the product (commerce).** The book talks about what the research supports. The product (programs, future supplements, membership) makes narrower commercial claims about itself specifically. A supplement cannot claim what the research literature claims; it can only claim what regulatory law allows a supplement to claim.

**Rule 3 — Brand voice does not perform the authors; founder voice may speak of them warmly and honestly.** The book's authors — Fred Bartlit, Steven Droullard, and Marni Boppart, ScD — are not active operators of StrongPath and the brand never performs them as if they were. Brand voice does not attribute current views to them, quote them as if they were speaking now, or imply ongoing involvement they do not have. Author names appear only on the two permitted surfaces per §5 (homepage book section credit line and Brief 10 credit line) and inside the book cover image. The one exception is Jeff's first-person language about Fred and Steven on the About page and homepage book section — that is Jeff speaking about his friends and the relay he is running, not brand-voice attribution of their positions. The distinction between Jeff's voice and brand voice on this point is the rule.

**Examples under all three rules:**

- ✅ Allowed (credibility frame): "StrongPath is the strength platform in the category defined by *Choosing the StrongPath: Reversing the Downward Spiral of Aging*, an Amazon bestseller in Aging, Weight Training, Exercise, and Longevity."
- ✅ Allowed (third-party substantiation): "Resistance training is the most effective intervention for age-related muscle loss. A 2011 review in *Current Opinion in Clinical Nutrition and Metabolic Care* concluded that progressive resistance training reversed muscle loss in adults over 65."
- ✅ Allowed (product framing that respects all three rules): "StrongPath programs translate current research on resistance training, protein intake, and recovery into protocols adults can follow at home."
- ✅ Allowed (Jeff's first-person voice on the About page): "Fred and Steven entrusted me with their legacy and asked me to carry the path they named to a wider audience."
- ❌ Prohibited (treats book as citation): "Research described in *Choosing the StrongPath* shows that resistance training is the most effective intervention." — Cite the research directly, not the book.
- ❌ Prohibited (brand voice performs the authors): "Fred Bartlit reminds us that strength training matters at every age." — The brand does not put words in the authors' mouths or attribute current views to them.
- ❌ Prohibited (product overclaim): "StrongPath reverses sarcopenia."
- ❌ Prohibited (book subtitle as our claim): "StrongPath reverses the downward spiral of aging."
- ❌ Prohibited (product unsubstantiated claim): "Our program is clinically proven to prevent muscle loss."
- ❌ Prohibited (future supplements): "StrongPath Protein prevents sarcopenia."

### 9.2 Verbs we use

**Allowed:** may support, may help maintain, is associated with, research suggests, some studies show, has been shown to (with citation), is consistent with.

**Prohibited:** cures, treats, prevents (for supplements specifically — FDA-regulated), reverses, eliminates, guarantees, is clinically proven (unless we have a clinical trial).

**Note on "reverse" / "reversing":** The word appears in the book's subtitle. It is allowed to appear inside the italicized book title when the book is cited. It is never used in our own voice.

### 9.3 Required disclaimers

Every page that discusses health interventions carries the BP-04 medical disclaimer verbatim:

> *This content is for informational and educational purposes only. It does not constitute medical advice and is not a substitute for professional medical consultation. Always consult your physician before beginning any new exercise or supplement program.*

Every page with affiliate links carries an FTC affiliate disclosure — at the top of the article, not buried in the footer.

Every piece of content mentioning supplements carries:

> *These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.*

### 9.4 Testimonials

Per BP-07, we never fabricate. When real testimonials are displayed, they include:
- Full first name and last initial
- Specific, quantified outcome with duration ("gained measurable strength over 12 weeks," not "changed my life")
- Disclosure if solicited or incentivized
- Disclaimer if above average: *"Results typical of dedicated program participants; individual results vary."*

### 9.5 The AI advisor

Per BP-05, the system prompt carries the full compliance block. Never diagnoses. Never recommends stopping medications. Never claims products treat, cure, or prevent disease. The Brand Ambassador reviews the advisor's system prompt before any change.

### 9.6 When in doubt, underpromise

**Explicit rule.** When the research is ambiguous, when an outcome varies widely by individual, or when a claim feels strong enough to need hedging — underpromise. Brands that underpromise and cite their sources earn the reader. Brands that overpromise get clicks and lose trust. Conversion pressure will push toward bigger claims; this rule exists to push back.

Practical applications:
- Prefer "may support muscle maintenance" over "supports muscle maintenance" when the evidence is mixed.
- Prefer "many participants report measurable strength gains in 8-12 weeks" over "gain strength in 8 weeks" as a timeline promise.
- Prefer naming a range ("most adults can safely start with…") over naming an absolute ("start with X pounds").
- When a competitor overclaims, do not match their claim. Cite ours accurately and let the comparison speak for itself.

### 9.7 Third-party sourcing policy

**Explicit rule.** Every specific claim in StrongPath content is substantiated by a current, named, third-party source. The book is not a substantiation source. The book is a credibility anchor for the brand as a whole; it does not carry individual claims in individual articles.

**Source priority order:**

1. **Peer-reviewed research** — cite with author, year, and journal. Example: "(Fiatarone et al., *NEJM*, 1994)" or "(Phillips et al., *Journal of Applied Physiology*, 2016)." Prefer recent research (past 10-15 years) where available; cite landmark older studies when they remain the standard reference.
2. **Named institutional sources** — Harvard Health Publishing, Mayo Clinic, NIH, CDC, American College of Sports Medicine, academic institutions. Free, credible, stable. Cite and link directly.
3. **The book — as credibility anchor only** — cited when the authority of the book itself is the relevant point (e.g., "*Choosing the StrongPath* is an Amazon bestseller in four categories"). Not cited to substantiate a specific health claim.
4. **Paid expert endorsements** — not used in Phase 1. Revisit in Phase 2 when budget permits retainers.
5. **Unsourced assertions** — not acceptable. If we cannot cite it, we rewrite it or cut it.

**Operational guidance for writers:**
- If a draft contains "the book shows that X" about a specific health claim, rewrite to cite the underlying research directly. Use the book's bibliography as a starting point for finding the right source, then verify the source is current and defensible on its own.
- If a draft cites the book and current research side-by-side in a way that suggests they are interchangeable sources, separate them. The book goes near the credibility framing ("the category-defining bestseller on this topic"); the research substantiates the claim.
- If a claim can only be substantiated by citing the book, the claim is not strong enough to make. Either find a current third-party source that supports it, or cut it.

## 10. The Brand Review Rubric

When reviewing any copy, email, ad, or creative, run through this checklist. A failure on any item is a rewrite.

### Voice check
- [ ] Sounds like the specialist, not the salesman
- [ ] Cites mechanisms, not miracles
- [ ] No forbidden words (anti-aging, miracle, breakthrough, cure, reverse, secret, hack, transform)
- [ ] "Reverse" / "reversing" appears only inside an italicized book title, never in our own voice
- [ ] No filler phrases (the truth is, here's the thing, let's face it)
- [ ] Short and long sentences mixed deliberately
- [ ] No talking down to the reader

### Tone check
- [ ] Primary audience identified before writing
- [ ] Tone matches the audience (peer-level / reassuring / professional)
- [ ] No use of "senior," "elderly," or "old" to describe the end user

### Authority check
- [ ] Book cited by title and bestseller status, not by author parenthetical, in article body and openers
- [ ] Author names appear only on the two permitted surfaces — homepage book section credit line and Brief 10 credit line
- [ ] Author names not present in article opener parentheticals, brand-voice prose, third-party citations, social bios, email signatures, or paid creative
- [ ] Book cover image is the only place author names appear visually outside the two permitted credit-line surfaces
- [ ] First-person founder language about Fred and Steven appears only on the About page and homepage book section, in Jeff's voice — not in brand-voice copy
- [ ] **Book is cited as credibility anchor, not as a substantiation source for specific health claims**
- [ ] **Every specific health claim is substantiated by a current, named third-party source (peer-reviewed study or institutional source), not by the book**
- [ ] Citation discipline visible — current peer-reviewed research, named institutional sources cited with author/year/journal where applicable
- [ ] Page is consistent with the platform-as-second-edition framing — current research, current language, no stale citations or 2018-era framing presented as current
- [ ] Jeff described as founder and operator, not clinician, scientist, or author

### Positioning check
- [ ] Content ladders to one of the four messaging pillars
- [ ] Serious, direct, hopeful — in that priority order
- [ ] Aligns with "pro-strength," not "anti-aging"

### Legal check
- [ ] Verbs are in the allowed set — no cures/treats/prevents/reverses (except inside the book's italicized title)
- [ ] The book's subtitle never appears as a claim in our own voice
- [ ] Medical disclaimer present on health pages
- [ ] FTC affiliate disclosure present above the fold on affiliate pages
- [ ] Amazon Associates tag `stron02-20` used on every Amazon affiliate link
- [ ] Testimonials (if any) are real and properly framed
- [ ] All three book/product firewall rules respected — book is credibility not citation, product claims stay narrower than research claims, brand voice does not perform the authors
- [ ] When in doubt, underpromise (Section 9.6) — apply before publishing

### Visual check (for design deliverables)
- [ ] Palette is navy-dominant, gold as accent
- [ ] Serif headline, sans-serif body
- [ ] Body copy minimum 18px desktop / 17px mobile
- [ ] No posed "active senior" stock photography
- [ ] White space generous; layout editorial, not commercial
- [ ] Book cover present on any substantive-claim page
