## Purpose

The seed vocabulary for every downstream SEO asset — pillar pages, satellite articles, internal linking architecture, paid-search negative lists, and AI-advisor topic coverage. Session 1 output. Session 2 turns selected clusters into article briefs. Session 3 turns briefs into the publishing calendar and internal linking map.

Not a commitment to write about every keyword. A map of the terrain.

---

## Method

Seed keywords derived from:
1. BRAND.md section 3.2 (approved vocabulary) and section 7 (four messaging pillars)
2. PERSONAS.md sections 5 (Margaret's words) and 5 (David's words, Before + After moments)
3. The book *Choosing the StrongPath* (vocabulary mined via BRAND.md and PERSONAS.md citations)
4. Google Autocomplete + People Also Ask on seed terms (manual SERP inspection)
5. Ahrefs Free Keyword Generator for volume estimates on head and mid-tail terms
6. AnswerThePublic free tier (3 searches) on the three highest-value seeds

**Volume estimates.** Where a cell reads `est. [N]/mo`, that's an Ahrefs Free or Keyword Planner estimate rounded to the nearest reasonable band. Where a cell reads `—`, volume was not confirmable in this session and will be validated before the keyword enters session 2's brief production. Volume data is directionally useful but not gospel — intent and competitive landscape matter more for Phase 1 ranking potential.

**Difficulty estimates.** Scored Low / Medium / High based on SERP competition (who ranks, domain authority, content depth). Low = realistic Phase 1 target. Medium = achievable with 3-6 months of sustained content. High = parking lot for Phase 2.

**FTC/FDA flag column.** Every keyword passed through the `ftc-fda-claim-review` skill. Flags: `Safe` (general wellness/education), `Caution` (structure-function adjacent — requires hedge discipline in the article), `Stop` (would require disease or treatment claim; rewrite or exclude).

**Explicitly excluded from this universe** (standing decisions, flagged here for transparency):
- David-Crisis queries — skilled nursing, Medicare rehab, hip-fracture recovery logistics, hospital discharge planning
- Dr. Chen / clinician-facing queries — Phase 2 activation
- "Anti-aging" / "longevity hack" / "biohacking" / "secret to…" vocabulary — BRAND.md voice violation regardless of volume
- Supplement-claim keywords where the obvious article lead would cross the book/product firewall
- Brand-conflict queries targeting competitors by name (covered by a separate paid-media plan, not organic)

The full excluded-keyword log is in Section 8.

## 2. Seed Terms by Messaging Pillar

24 seeds. These are the starting points for topical expansion. Most downstream keywords ladder up to one of these.

### Pillar 1 — Sarcopenia is real, measurable, and largely preventable

Educational backbone. Defines the problem. The primary Pillar 1 head term, `sarcopenia`, is high-difficulty (Cleveland Clinic, Mayo Clinic, NIA all rank) but the mid-tail and question-format variants are winnable. Strategy: build a comprehensive pillar page on `what is sarcopenia` that outranks thin informational content, then satellite articles on mechanism, measurement, and stage-specific response.

1. sarcopenia
2. age-related muscle loss
3. muscle loss with aging
4. dynapenia
5. how to prevent muscle loss as you age
6. signs of sarcopenia

### Pillar 2 — Strength is the operating system of independence

Reframes strength from aesthetic to functional. Outcome is not abs — it is carrying groceries at 75, walking a mile at 85, staying in your own home. High emotional-weight pillar. Keywords here skew toward functional-outcome queries (falls, balance, independence) rather than aesthetic queries (toning, weight loss, body composition). Caution: several seeds here brush fall-prevention territory, which is closer to disease-claim territory per the skill (flagged individually).

7. strength training after 60
8. how to stay strong as you age
9. strength training for seniors (used for David's father — not Margaret)
10. functional strength for older adults
11. how to keep your independence as you age
12. balance and strength for aging

### Pillar 3 — The research is clear; the execution is the hard part

Most readers have heard "lift weights and eat protein." Our job: package it into a protocol a 65-year-old can follow. Keywords here skew execution-focused: protocols, programs, protein targets, progression, form. High commercial intent (these readers are close to buying a program). Also the pillar that will eventually carry the AI advisor and program pages.

13. strength training protocol for older adults
14. how much protein for adults over 60
15. resistance training over 60
16. how to start lifting weights at 60
17. progressive overload for older adults
18. best exercises for sarcopenia

### Pillar 4 — You do not walk this alone

Community and guidance pillar. Almost entirely David territory in Phase 1 (Margaret searches are self-oriented; David searches are parent-oriented or family-oriented). Carries caregiver content, family-training content, and eventually the membership. Lowest-volume pillar but highest conversion intent for David.

19. how to help my parents stay strong
20. how to get my elderly parent to exercise
21. exercise program for aging parents
22. how to talk to a parent about getting stronger
23. caregiver resources for aging parent strength
24. family fitness for aging parents

## 6. Long-Tail / Question-Format Bank

Pulled from Google's People Also Ask, AnswerThePublic (3 searches on `sarcopenia`, `strength training over 60`, `how to help aging parents`), and Autocomplete expansion. These are the FAQ-section gold mines. Most are low-volume but convert because the intent is precise.

**Sarcopenia questions (Margaret + David):**
- What are the early warning signs of sarcopenia?
- Can sarcopenia be reversed? (**Caution** — rewrite angle to "what research shows about slowing and partially recovering from age-related muscle loss")
- Is sarcopenia a disease? (**Caution** — it has ICD-10 code M62.84 since 2016; write educationally, careful about product claims)
